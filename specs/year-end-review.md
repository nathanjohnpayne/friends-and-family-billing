# Year-End Review

Covers the guided page through which a new billing year is created: the closing year's bill changes (True-ups and Service Credits), subscriptions that started mid-year, member charges, the per-household preview, the atomic rollover that appends the review's records to the closing year and seeds the new year with an itemized carry, the local draft, the next-year guard, and the member-facing "Carried from &lt;year&gt;" section. Decisions: [ADR 0009](../docs/adr/0009-year-end-review-true-ups-and-itemized-carry.md). Wireframes: `plans/ffb-wireframes.html` (`#fx-year-end-…`, `#fx-settings-populated-…`, `#fx-dashboard-yearend-…`, `#fx-share-carried-…`, `#fx-invoicing-preview-carried-…`, `#fx-dlg-email-invoice-full-carried-…`, `#fx-dlg-bill-history-rollover-…`).

Out of scope for v1: standalone mid-year price changes or late starts (the pure builders stay callable), mid-year membership changes on a bill, frequency changes as part of a price change, day-level proration or non-calendar years, new UI for off-cycle billing True-ups, effective-dated pricing or price history on the bill model.

## Approved rounding amendment (#629)

[ADR 0010](../docs/adr/0010-household-cent-projection.md) is the authoritative exact-input, six-component cent projection, row-allocation, integrity, epsilon-retirement and public money-version contract. It supersedes the earlier raw/epsilon decision boundary and only that part of the `calculations.js` freeze. The fixture matrix there and [serial rollout plan](../plans/629-cent-rounding-rollout.md) are prerequisites for #603. This documentation adopts the owner-approved contract before code; it does not claim implementation or authorize the live read-only flip report, which needs separate owner authorization or owner execution before Hosting.

The [owner's finite-Number decision](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/friends-and-family-billing/issues/633#issuecomment-6690912942) requires every admitted source money field, across G/S/O/P/R/B and deferred-charge/seed/item/detail projections, to satisfy `typeof value === 'number' && Number.isFinite(value)`. Numeric strings, `NaN`, infinities, `null`, `undefined` and booleans put only the affected household on an integrity hold, naming household, record id, field and observed type. The hold blocks settlement, caps, close, carry and public money projection; records still participate in presence signals such as the refund warning. Recorders keep their write-time parsing; corrections go through the app and write a Number, without migration or stored conversion. Sign/domain exclusions require the [documented inventory](../plans/629-cent-rounding-rollout.md#source-sign-and-domain-guard-inventory); silent drops default to holds unless documented product behavior says otherwise.

M2a (#633) supplies unused exact money-edge replacements and old/new boundary fixtures for `toCents`, `snapshotExistingCredit`, override/existing-credit handling in `compute` / `computePriceChange` / `computeEnded` / `computeLateStart`, `buildUsageChargeRecord`, `buildHouseholdPreview` and `validateDraft`. It leaves their active legacy callers untouched, preserves exact `decimalCoefficient` / `proratedCents`, per-record credit matching and source/draft binding, and retains stored `splitCents`. M2b (#634) performs coherent activation and replaces the active S/B leading-numeric-string test locks with hold assertions: no coerced-money assertion or legacy display authority remains after that activation. Before M3/M4 start, define how their surfaces render household holds. M5 (#637) counts non-conforming-field holds by component with record ids in its read-only report; implementing that report authorizes no live read.

## Acceptance Criteria

### Vocabulary and units

- Administrator-facing copy (the review page, billing events, the bill history dialog) may say True-up. Member-facing copy (share page, invoice preview, invoice email, text invoice) never says True-up, carry_opening, reconciliation, or adjustment; the approved section heading is **Carried from &lt;year&gt;** and every line says what happened and when (see [Member-facing copy](#member-facing-copy)).
- **Money:** existing persisted dollar numbers, including legacy sub-cent values, are never normalized. New review records use integer cents converted once to dollars. `toCents` in the formulas below means ADR 0010 exact decimal/rational quantization with half-away-from-zero ties and signed-zero normalization, never `Number.EPSILON`. Household outcomes compose the six once-quantized components from that ADR; stored `splitCents` and projected largest remainder are distinct rules.
- **Signs.** An `owedAdjustments[]` record's `amount` is a positive magnitude; `kind` gives the direction (`usage_charge` raises owed, `service_credit` lowers it). A `carry_opening` seed's `amount` is signed (a carried charge positive, a carried credit negative), as today. `items[].amount` and `detail[].amount` are signed the same way (charge `+`, credit `−`). Preview and display totals are signed; the UI renders `+$12.00` and `−$26.00`.
- `calc` stores the amounts the administrator entered in the bill's own frequency plus the derived monthly figures: `{ frequency: 'monthly' | 'annual', fromAmount, toAmount, fromMonthly, toMonthly, firstMonth, lastMonth, months, computed, gross, overridden, recorded, coveredBy }`. The three amounts are defined once: `computed` is the formula's result; **`gross` is the bill-level amount after any override and before existing-credit netting**, the amount the split is of; `recorded` is `Σ net_m`, what is actually written, with `gross − recorded = Σ coveredBy[].appliedAmount`. A per-member record adds `calc.share` (its share of `gross`) and `calc.applied` (the amount its matched credits covered). `fromMonthly`/`toMonthly` are `amount` for a monthly bill and `amount / 12` **unrounded** for an annual bill; `firstMonth` / `lastMonth` are `YYYY-MM` strings inside the source year (`lastMonth` only for `basis: 'ended'`, `fromAmount`/`fromMonthly` null for `basis: 'late_start'`); `months` is the count the formula used; `computed` is the formula's result in dollars; `gross` equals `computed` unless `overridden: true`; the per-member shares sum exactly to `gross`, and the per-member records written sum exactly to `recorded`.

### True-up and Service Credit math (`src/lib/year-end-review.js`, pure)

- `monthsFromFirst(firstMonth)` returns `13 − month(firstMonth)` (July → 6, January → 12, December → 1). `monthsAfterLast(lastMonth)` returns `12 − month(lastMonth)` (August → 4, December → 0). Both reject a month outside the source year.
- **Price change.** `grossCents = toCents((toMonthly − fromMonthly) × months)` with `months = monthsFromFirst(firstMonth)`. A positive result is a True-up (`kind: 'usage_charge'`, `basis: 'price_change'`); a negative result is a Service Credit (`kind: 'service_credit'`, `basis: 'price_change'`) of the absolute value. For an annual bill `toMonthly − fromMonthly = (toAmount − fromAmount) / 12`, multiplied by `months` **before** rounding: the annual-increase fixture is $16.67, not $16.70. The review computes the entered prices as exact decimal coefficients from their `Number.toString()` representations, subtracts and multiplies months exactly, then divides by 12 for an annual bill at the final symmetric cents-rounding step; binary floating-point subtraction must not turn a half-cent result into a lower amount. The monthly figures in `calc` remain unrounded display numbers.
- **Late start.** `grossCents = toCents(toMonthly × months)` with `months = monthsFromFirst(firstMonth)`; a True-up with `basis: 'late_start'`.
- **Ended.** `grossCents = toCents(fromMonthly × monthsAfterLast(lastMonth))`; a Service Credit with `basis: 'ended'`. The last month of service is included in what was used; the credit covers the following month through December.
- **Existing Service Credits on the bill (decided 2026-11-05, D-B option 1).** A bill marked Ended or Price changed (downward) may already carry active Service Credits recorded during the year from the Bills tab or a Review Request resolution. Each is a per-member record (`recordServiceCredit` writes one record per positive-share member, each with its own id; `billId`, `memberId`, `amount`, free-text `reason`, `incurredDate`, `createdAt`, `status: 'active'`, no period, no basis, no group id). The row lists **every such record** under "Credits already on this bill in 2026" with its date, reason, member and amount, grouped visually by reason and date for reading only, and requires an explicit answer per record with **no default**, from three: **Covers this change** (the whole record), **Covers in part…** (an entered amount), or **Unrelated**. Next is disabled until every listed record has a valid answer ("Say whether Ada's $3.00 credit from Sep 2 covers this change."). Choices are stored and restored by the record's actual adjustment id; the page never infers a shared group, a period, or a purpose the record does not carry, and never edits, voids or re-dates an existing credit. **Netting is per member and applies only the amount that covers this change:** with `share_m` the member's share of the computed (or overridden) credit and `remaining_m = share_m − Σ applied by the member's other answered records`, a record of amount `A` may be answered *Covers* only when `A ≤ remaining_m`, and then `applied = A` (when `A > remaining_m` *Covers* is disabled with the reason "larger than <member>'s remaining $X"); *Covers in part…* is offered for **every** record, because a record smaller than the share can still be mixed-purpose (a $10.00 credit of which $5.00 was the cancellation and $5.00 an outage) and the data cannot tell, and it asks for the covering amount (`0 < applied < A` and `applied ≤ remaining_m`, no default; a full amount is the *Covers* answer) and stops until it is entered; *Unrelated* applies nothing. The page never assumes a whole record applies. `net_m = share_m − Σ applied_m` (never negative by construction); the recorded gross is `Σ net_m`, one record per member with `net_m > 0`, and the row previews the resulting credit per member before anything is recorded ("Ada $1.00 · Erik $1.00 · Kenji $1.00 · Dede $1.00 · Rosa $0.00 → $4.00 across 4 members"). When every member nets to zero the row records nothing and says what still happens: an **Ended** bill is left out of the next year ("Nothing more to record; the bill is left out of 2027"), a **Price changed** bill is kept at its new price ("…; 2027 uses $24.00"). **Audit:** the applied ids and amounts are kept whether or not a record is written: each recorded Service Credit carries `calc.coveredBy: [{ adjustmentId, memberId, appliedAmount }]` for its member, the `SERVICE_CREDIT_RECORDED` event carries the bill's full `coveredBy[]`, and a bill that records nothing writes a `SERVICE_CREDIT_COVERED` event with the same `calc` and `coveredBy[]` (§ Rollover). **Revalidation:** an answer is kept only while the record it was given for and the change it was given against are unchanged (§ Draft); a restored draft never reuses a stale answer silently.
- **Split.** `splitCents(totalCents, memberIds)` is one shared helper, extracted from `recordServiceCredit` with its exact behavior: `base = Math.floor(totalCents / n)`, every member but the last gets `base`, the last gets `totalCents − base × (n − 1)`, so the shares sum exactly to `totalCents`. **This stored last-member-remainder rule is retained; only projected additive rows use ADR 0010 largest remainder. A share of 0 cents produces no record:** one cent across three members yields one $0.01 record for the last member and none for the other two; the audit event still lists every member with their share, zeros included, and no zero-value deferred or credit record is ever written. The ordinary `recordServiceCredit` writer uses that same split unchanged and appends only positive-share records. Its existing `SERVICE_CREDIT_RECORDED` event retains every target, including zero shares, as `payload.shares: [{ memberId, amount }]`, and `memberCount` counts all targets. This is future-write conformance only: existing stored zero Service Credits remain subject to the ADR 0010 source integrity hold; no record is corrected, dropped or migrated.
- **Override.** The administrator may replace the computed gross amount; the record keeps `calc.computed`, sets `calc.gross` to the override and `calc.overridden: true`, and the split (and any per-member netting against matched existing credits) is of the override. An override must be a positive amount in the same direction as the formula: a True-up cannot be overridden into a credit (use a Service Credit row instead) and **there is no override of 0**. A recorded amount of 0 is never typed; it arises only from the two zero cases below.
- **Zero cases are explicit, and the outcome depends on the change, not on the amount.** Four situations record nothing: `months = 0` (a bill ended in December), every member netting to zero against matched existing credits, a bill with no assigned members (below), and a real price change whose gross rounds to 0 cents (an annual bill that changes by a few cents late in the year): the row says "Rounds to $0.00—nothing to record" and the bill is kept at its new price. In each, an **Ended** bill is still left out of the new year and a **Price changed** bill still appears in the new year at its new price; the row states which ("Nothing more to record; the bill is left out of 2027" / "Nothing more to record; 2027 uses $24.00"). A price change whose new price equals the current price is a validation error ("No change—leave the bill Unchanged or enter a different price"), never a zero-amount record. An override of 0 or less is a validation error. A late-start bill with no members is a validation error. **A third no-record case is an existing bill with no assigned members** (`addBill` creates bills with `members: []` and the last member can be removed): marked Ended or Price changed it records nothing, because nobody was billed for it, is not an error, and the row says "No members on this bill—nothing to credit or charge"; an Ended memberless bill is left out of the new year, a Price changed one is kept at its new price, and neither blocks Start (fixture 8j). `calc.months` and the price are still recorded on the row for the audit event (`SERVICE_CREDIT_COVERED` is not written; a `BILL_ENDED_UNASSIGNED { billId, billName, lastMonth, reconciliationId }` note is).
- **Formula strings** shown beside every computed amount: price change `($198.00 − $180.00) × 6 months = $108.00 ÷ 9 members = $12.00 each`; annual `($159.00 − $139.00) ÷ 12 × 10 months = $16.67 ÷ 3 members = $5.55 · $5.55 · $5.57`; late start `$9.99 × 4 months = $39.96 ÷ 3 members = $13.32 each`; ended `$12.00 × 4 months (Sep–Dec) = $48.00 ÷ 3 members = $16.00 each`. An overridden row shows `computed $108.00 → entered $100.00`.

### Record shapes

- **True-up records** (one per member of the bill): `{ id: 'oadj_tu_<sourceYearId>_<trueUpId>_<memberId>', trueUpId, memberId, billId, billName, kind: 'usage_charge', basis: 'price_change' | 'late_start', amount, description, incurredDate, calc, status, createdAt, reconciliationId }`. `trueUpId` is `tu_<sourceYearId>_<billId>` for a price change and `tu_<sourceYearId>_new_<newBillId>` for a late start. `description` is the member-facing sentence (below). `incurredDate` is the first day of `firstMonth`. `status` is `'deferred'` in the in-memory reconciliation and `'carried_forward'` in the first persisted state (ADR 0009 § 3). A Usage Charge record with no `basis` is treated as `basis: 'member'` everywhere.
- **Review Service Credit records**: the existing `recordServiceCredit` shape plus `basis: 'price_change' | 'ended'`, `calc` (with `calc.coveredBy: [{ adjustmentId, memberId, appliedAmount }]` listing the member's existing credits the administrator marked as covering this change, possibly empty), `reconciliationId`, `id: 'oadj_sc_<sourceYearId>_<basis>_<billId>_<memberId>'`, `reason` set to the member-facing sentence, `status: 'active'`. A member whose share is fully covered gets no record; the coverage lives in the events.
- **Member charges** added in Step 3: the existing `recordUsageCharge` shape (`basis` absent) with `id: 'oadj_uc_<sourceYearId>_<rowId>'` and `reconciliationId`, where `rowId` is allocated once when the row is created in the draft and stored on it. Existing deferred charges shown in Step 3 are reused by id and never re-recorded.
- **New bills** in Step 2 allocate `billId` once with `generateUniqueBillId(existingIds)` when the row is created and store it on the draft; the True-up's `billId` and the new year's bill use that id. On draft restoration a `billId` that collides with a current source-year bill is reallocated and the row is flagged for re-check.
- **The carried credit** record and its seed credit item use the same actual id finalized before planning. `buildCarryMoneySnapshot` accepts caller-selected `creditDispositionIds`; when omitted, its pure-helper default is `cadj_carry_<nextYearLabel>_<primaryMemberId>`. Ordinary legacy `createYear` supplies generated `generateCreditAdjustmentId()` values, preserving its random ids. The upcoming reviewed rollover retains ADR 0009’s approved deterministic/idempotent `cadj_carry_<nextYearLabel>_<primaryMemberId>` ids, supplied in that finalized map before validation. `applyCarryForwardToPriorYear` generates no ids and rejects a late `idFactory`; neither path validates deterministic stand-ins and substitutes different ids afterward.
- **New persisted fields** on `owedAdjustments[]` records (`basis`, `calc`, `trueUpId`, `billName`, `reconciliationId`) and on `carry_opening` seeds (`items[]`) ride the verbatim array round-trip of `buildSavePayload`; no allowlist change (ADR 0008). Completion evidence lives in `billingEvents[]`, also already in the allowlist.
- No change to `storage.rules`. `firestore.rules` changes as § Versioned year-document writes specifies (decided 2026-11-05, D-A): version-checked writes on `users/{uid}/billingYears/{yearId}`, delete denied, staged before the reviewed rollover; nothing else in the rules changes.

### Itemized carry (`src/lib/billing-year.js`)

- `buildCarryOpeningItems(household, sourceYear)` clones the finalized household seed’s allocated `items[]`: one item per carried deferred Usage Charge (its new snapshot amount is the ADR 0010 allocated source-row cents divided by 100; the original charge record is unchanged) `{ sourceId: <charge id>, kind: 'usage_charge', basis, memberId, billName, description, amount: +allocatedChargeCents / 100, incurredDate, calc }` (True-ups included, `basis: 'price_change' | 'late_start'`; member charges `basis: 'member'`), and, when the household carries a Credit, exactly one `{ sourceId: <actual finalized carry disposition id>, kind: 'credit', basis: 'credit', memberId: <primary>, billName: null, description: 'Your household's <year> credit', amount: −(credit + unusedOpeningCredit), detail: [...] }` (when only the unused incoming credit carries, `sourceId` is the source seed's id, since no `carry_forward` record exists). `calculations.js` may change only at the ADR 0010 projection boundary; the formula and eligibility remain unchanged.
- `detail[]` provenance is finalized inside the same carry plan. The review path supplies its own Service Credit records as `options.reviewServiceCredits` before planning (the records built for this `reconciliationId`), and the adapter selects only admitted records from that household/component. `buildCarryOpeningItems` only clones the resulting seed items; its later context cannot change amounts, ids or detail. Earlier Service Credits remain in the remainder line. Review-only unused incoming credit comes from the plan’s allocated `U`; the legacy non-itemized path stores no detail.
- `detail[]` on the credit item is the household credit's reconciliation: the review's Service Credits for the household's members `{ sourceId, basis, memberId, billName, description, amount: −share }`; the unused incoming credit line `{ kind: 'unused', description: 'Unused <year> credit', amount: −U }` when `U > 0` (below); and one closing line when the running sum still differs from the item: `{ kind: 'remainder', description: 'Other <year> overpayment', amount: −(credit − Σ Service Credits) }` when the payment-backed credit exceeds the review's Service Credits, or `{ kind: 'applied', description: 'Part applied to your <year> balance', amount: +(Σ Service Credits − credit) }` when the Service Credits exceed it. **`Σ detail[].amount` equals the credit item's `amount`**; detail is never added to the seed, to `items[]`, or to any total (the item is).
- **Invariants (property-tested over generated fixtures: random households, payments, refunds, incoming seeds, charges, credits, True-ups, Service Credits).** (a) For every seed, projected `Σ itemCents == seedCents` and projected `Σ detailCents == creditItemCents` exactly; validate raw parent/child invariants and allocate through the hierarchy as ADR 0010 defines. All conservation/settlement comparisons below are exact integer-cent comparisons. (b) **Conservation:** for every household, `seed.amount = deferredChargeTotal − (credit + U)` where `credit` is the household's `getHouseholdFinancials` credit in the source year after the review's records and `U` is its unused incoming credit; nothing else enters or leaves. (c) **Old-year settlement:** after the commit, every household in the source year has `credit = 0` **as `buildCarryForward` computes it** (`getHouseholdFinancials` with `reopenedAdjustmentIds = null`, which subtracts every recorded refund and carry, re-opened ones included), and its shortfall (`owed − netContribution`) is exactly 0 when it carried a payment-backed credit and **unchanged** otherwise; the carry never creates debt in the source year. **Re-opened refunds are the one deliberate exception to "no Credit left behind":** a refund re-opened by an active `not_received` (#319, ADR 0003) is money owed back in the source year, so the carry never includes it (`buildCarryForward` passes `null` for exactly that reason), the settlement board, which passes the active re-opened set, still shows that household a Credit equal to the re-opened refund after the rollover, the `not_received` stays live there, and the review's Step 4 says so for the household ("$30.00 refund still to be re-sent in 2026; not carried"). Fixture 10m. (d) Over a chain of rollovers with no payments and no bills, the carried credit is identical in every year (fixture 10f).
- Each item snapshots `billName` and `description` at rollover; renderers never look up the new year's bill names or prices for carried items.
- `buildNewYearData(..., carry, fromYearLabel)` writes `items` on each seed when the carry supplies them; a carry without items (the legacy call) seeds exactly as today. Seeds written before this change have no `items`. **Zero net:** when a household's deferred charges and credit cancel (`buildCarryForward` keeps the household, the source year marks the charge `carried_forward` and writes the `carry_forward` record, and `openingBalanceCents == 0` exactly), the review path still writes the seed with `amount: 0` and the household's `items[]`, so the carried lines are explained and invariant (a) holds; the legacy call skips a zero balance as today. Fixture 10j.
- Attribution: a True-up or member charge item is attributed to its `memberId` (a linked member's item shows under that member within the household); the credit item is attributed to the household, never to one member as though it were that member's charge.
- **Incoming credit is money; a Service Credit is not.** `getHouseholdFinancials` floors the bill-derived owed at 0 (`owed = max(0, grossOwed − serviceCreditTotal + priorOpeningBalance) + billedChargeTotal`) and `credit = max(0, netContribution − owed)`. Two different things disappear in that floor and must be treated differently. **An over-large Service Credit** (credits exceeding the bills) is a reduction of charges with no money behind it: the existing rule forfeits the excess ("neither an over-large credit nor a carried credit can make the bills owe the member money"), nothing is carried for it, and the review keeps that rule (fixture 10e). **An incoming credit** (a negative `carry_opening` seed) is money the household paid in an earlier year, disposed there by a `carry_forward` record; when it exceeds the year's charges the floor hides the remainder, `credit` reads 0, and `buildCarryForward` would drop it at the next rollover (the #322 edge case). The review path preserves it. Definitions, in cents, from the values `getHouseholdFinancials` returns for the household: `I = max(0, −priorOpeningBalance)` (incoming credit), `Q = max(0, priorOpeningBalance)` (incoming charge), `A = max(0, grossOwed − serviceCreditTotal + Q)` (charges after Service Credits, the existing floor), **`U = max(0, I − A)`** (unused incoming credit). Using these integer-cent components, the household's ledger balance is `netContribution + I − A − billedChargeTotal = (netContribution − owed) + U`, so `U` is exactly the part of the money the floor hides. `buildCarryForwardSummary(..., { carryUnusedOpeningCredit: true })` (review path only; the legacy call keeps its existing carry eligibility, with ADR 0010 cents) adds, for every household with `U > 0` integer cents (including households `buildCarryForward` omitted because they had no credit and no deferred charge), `unusedOpeningCredit: U` and `openingSeedIds` to the household entry and subtracts `U` from its `openingBalance`; `credit` is **not** changed, so `applyCarryForwardToPriorYear` still writes a `carry_forward` record only for the payment-backed credit. **The unused incoming credit is carried without a disposition record**: a `carry_forward` record subtracts from `netContribution`, and `U` was never in `netContribution`, so disposing it that way would turn the source year's Settled household into one Outstanding by `U` (the finding's second example). Instead `applyCarryForwardToPriorYear` stamps `carriedForwardTo: <nextYearLabel>` on the household's source `carry_opening` seeds (status stays `carried_in`; `getHouseholdOpeningBalance` reads only `amount` and `status !== 'voided'`, so the source year's owed, credit and status are identical before and after). `buildCarryOpeningItems` makes the credit item `amount: −(credit + U)` with the `unused` detail line. `calculations.js` may change only at the ADR 0010 projection boundary; the formula and eligibility remain unchanged. Because `credit` is gated on `netContribution`, the Record Refund path never sees `U`: under the preserved owed formula an unused incoming credit can only carry, never be refunded in the year it was unused, and recording a refund against it would create a shortfall of the same amount. ADR 0004 says a credit is never trapped because refund is the fallback, so whether v1 accepts this limitation (carry only, stated on the member page) or changes `src/lib/calculations.js` so an incoming credit counts in Net Contribution is a product decision, filed as D-C (#617, § Decisions pending); the design below assumes carry-only until it is decided. Billed Usage Charges are never offset by it (#320): a household with an unpaid billed charge and an unused incoming credit carries `U` and keeps its shortfall in the source year (fixture 10h). Member-facing surfaces show a $0.00 total for a household whose carried credit exceeds its bills, with the line "Your <year> credit of $X covers this year; $Y remains.", where `$Y` is this same `U` and never the floor remainder (a Service Credit excess is shown as "Not applied this year" and is not promised as remaining).
- Fixtures 9 and 10 (§ Worked examples) are the unit fixtures for this rule: the excess-credit chain, and the conservation set (unpaid, partially paid, overpaid, already refunded, over-large Service Credit, no bills, partly used incoming credit, an unpaid billed charge, and an incoming credit with a review Service Credit).

### Next-year bills

- `deriveNextYearBills(sourceBills, reviewBills, newBills)` returns the new year's `bills[]`: every source bill not marked Ended, with `amount` set to its next-year price (defaulting to the new price when the price changed, otherwise the current price; always editable), the same `billingFrequency`, members, logo, and website; plus each new subscription as a bill with the draft's `billId`, name, amount, frequency, website, and members. Ended bills are absent.
- The source year's `bills[]` is never changed: no amount edits, no late-start bills added.
- A next-year price must be a positive number in the bill's frequency; an increase announced for January is entered here without a True-up.

### Household preview (Step 4)

- `buildHouseholdPreview(...)` returns, per household (primary plus linked members, ADR 0001): `{ primaryMemberId, members: [{ memberId, sourceBillTotal, nextBillTotal }], sourceBillTotal, nextBillTotal, subscriptionDelta, items: [...], carriedTotal, firstInvoiceTotal, remainingCredit }` (`remainingCredit` is the unused incoming credit of § Member-facing surfaces, computed before the new year has any Service Credit, so it equals `max(0, −(nextBillTotal + carriedTotal))` there) where `sourceBillTotal` is the household's **bill-derived annual total in the closing year** (ADR 0010 `G`, reconstructed exactly from source bill fields before quantization and before any adjustment), `nextBillTotal` is the same projected component over the next-year bills, `subscriptionDelta = nextBillTotal − sourceBillTotal`, `carriedTotal = Σ items[].amount` (the seed's net amount, the one number every "Carried" figure shows; where a charge/credit split is displayed it is **derived from the net**, `carriedCharges = Σ positive items` and `carriedCredit = carriedTotal − carriedCharges`, never two independent sums, so the split always reconciles to the seed), `firstInvoiceTotal = max(0, nextBillTotal + carriedTotal)` (the floor mirrors `getHouseholdFinancials`), and `remainingCredit = max(0, −(nextBillTotal + carriedTotal))`, the part of a carried credit the new year's bills do not use (shown as "$Y remains" and preserved at the following rollover by the rule above).
- The preview labels its baseline explicitly: "2026 bills" (what the household was billed for 2026 subscriptions) versus "2027 bills" (next year's subscriptions at their new prices). It never compares an unpaid balance with an annual billed cost, and it shows **Subscriptions vs 2026** separately from **Carried from 2026** so a catch-up charge is never hidden inside a price change.
- Every computed number shows its formula on demand; nothing is written until Start.

### The page (`/year-end-review`, `src/app/views/YearEndReview/YearEndReviewView.jsx`)

- A page under the app shell, not a dialog. Four steps with a step indicator; each step validates on Next and shows errors inline; Back never loses input. Steps 1–3 can be skipped only through the explicit "Nothing changed" confirmations (below).
- **Step 1 — Bills.** Every source bill, defaulting to **Unchanged**, with a one-click **No price changes this year**. Per row: Price changed (new price in the bill's frequency, first month billed at it, the computed True-up or Service Credit with formula and per-member split, amount overridable), Ended (last month of service, the computed Service Credit, "left out of 2027"), the **Credits already on this bill** list with its per-record Covers / Covers in part… / Unrelated answers and the per-member preview when the bill has active Service Credits (§ True-up and Service Credit math), and **Next year's price** for every bill (default current or new price). A price change from January shows the hint "This bills all twelve months at the new price."
- **Step 2 — New subscriptions.** Empty by default and fast to skip. A row: name, price, frequency, website (optional, for the logo), members, first month of service; shows the late-start True-up and the next-year bill it creates.
- **Step 3 — Member charges.** Per household, the existing deferred Usage Charges prefilled (read-only, "already recorded") and **Add charge** with the existing Usage Charge validation (positive amount, description required, member of the household). Charges added here are recorded in the batch, not immediately.
- **Step 4 — Review & start &lt;year&gt;.** Per household: 2026 bills, 2027 bills at their new prices with the subscription delta, carried items grouped by member with the household credit (and its detail) at the household level, the carried total, the first-invoice total; each household expandable; Edit links back to Steps 1–3. Then the year label (from `suggestNextYearLabel`, editable, duplicate-checked with `isYearLabelDuplicate`), a plain-language statement of exactly what Start will do (counts of True-ups, Service Credits, member charges, new bills, price changes, ended bills, households carrying, and the two documents written), and **Start &lt;year&gt;**. **Start is enabled only when every step validates** — including every existing-credit answer being present and current — and Step 4 has been recomputed since the last edit; while anything blocks it, Start is disabled with the reason, the blocked items are listed with links to their step, and the figures and household totals are marked *pending* rather than shown as final.
- **Nothing changed** is a fast path that requires three explicit confirmations: no closing-year bill changes, no new subscriptions, no additional member charges. "No price changes" alone does not skip Steps 2 and 3. It skips the editing of Steps 1–3; Step 4 still shows every existing deferred charge, Credit, next-year price (defaulting to the current price), and resulting total, with links back to edit. It persists no new financial records merely because a row was prefilled; prefilled deferred Usage Charges are carried once, never re-recorded.
- Entry points: Settings → Billing year (**Review & start &lt;next year&gt;**, replacing Start New Year and its label dialog) whenever the guard below finds no successor and the status permits, regardless of the date; and a Dashboard prompt card shown when **no successor exists AND (the year is Closed OR today is on or after November 1 of the year's four-digit numeric label)**. Cases: no successor, Open or Settling, before November 1 → no card (Settings still offers the review); no successor, Open or Settling, November 1 or later (including any later year) → card; no successor, Closed, any date → card; Archived → never; a successor exists → never, whatever the status or date. A label that is not four digits ("2026-27") has no date trigger and uses the Closed trigger only. "Today" is the browser's local date (`localDateString()`), the convention incurred dates already use.
- Guard: `findSuccessorYear(billingYears, sourceYear, sourceEvents)` returns a successor when (1) the source year's `billingEvents` contain a `YEAR_ROLLED_OVER` or `YEAR_CARRIED_FORWARD` event (its `toYear`), or (2) a billing year exists whose numeric label is greater than the source's numeric label; otherwise null. The review is offered only when there is no successor and the source status is `open`, `settling`, or `closed` (never `archived`). The guard runs at entry and again at Start after `_loadYearsList()`, and the commit precondition is the final check. When a next year exists the page says so and points to recording new charges in the open year (ADR 0007).

### Draft (local only)

- Draft state is component state mirrored to `localStorage` under `ffb.yearEndReview.v1.<uid>.<sourceYearId>`; every read and write is wrapped in `try/catch`; the billing-year document is never written before Start (ADR 0008 untouched).
- Shape: `{ draftVersion: 1, reconciliationId, userId, sourceYearId, sourceLabel, nextYearLabel, sourceFingerprint, savedAt, bills: { [billId]: { change: 'unchanged' | 'price_change' | 'ended', newAmount, firstMonth, lastMonth, override, nextYearAmount, existingCredits: { [adjustmentId]: { choice: 'covers' | 'partial' | 'unrelated', appliedCents, seen: { memberId, amountCents, incurredDate, reason, createdAt, status }, coverage: { basis, frequency, fromAmount, toAmount, firstMonth, lastMonth, override, shareCents } } } } }, newBills: [{ rowId, billId, name, amount, frequency, website, members, firstMonth, override }], charges: [{ rowId, memberId, amount, description, incurredDate }], confirmations: { noBillChanges, noNewSubscriptions, noExtraCharges } }`. `reconciliationId` is allocated once when the draft is created and never changes through restoration or retries. Derived amounts (True-ups, Service Credits, items, totals) are never stored.
- `validateDraft(raw, { userId, sourceYear, owedAdjustments, bills, familyMembers, payments, creditAdjustments, billingEvents })` — where `owedAdjustments` is the source year's current adjustment array, read so that every existing-credit answer can be checked against its record by adjustment id — takes the current `payments`, `creditAdjustments`, and `billingEvents` explicitly for fingerprint comparison (these override arrays embedded in `sourceYear`; a full source-year snapshot remains supported as a fallback). Callers pass the same live financial arrays used for the reviewed fingerprint. It treats storage as untrusted input: the shape and every field type are checked; a draft for another user, another source year, or another `draftVersion` is incompatible and discarded with a message; referenced bills and members are resolved against current data (a bill that no longer exists drops its row with a notice; a member no longer on a bill or in the household drops that charge with a notice); amounts and months are re-validated; derived amounts are recomputed. Restoration preserves every `rowId`, `billId`, and the `reconciliationId`. **Existing-credit answers are revalidated, never reused blindly:** an answer is kept only when the record with that adjustment id still exists and is active and its `memberId`, `amount`, `incurredDate`, `reason` and `createdAt` equal the stored `seen`, and when the change's `coverage` — the complete bill change: basis, frequency, the from and to amounts, the first or last month, the override, and the member's share in cents — is unchanged (a change from $100 → $120 to $110 → $130 keeps the share but not the coverage, so the answer is cleared); otherwise the answer is cleared, the row is flagged ("Answer again: Dede's $10.00 credit changed" / "Answer again: the months this change covers changed") and Next is disabled until it is re-answered. A record that no longer exists drops its answer with a notice; a record recorded since the draft was saved appears unanswered. Editing the bill's price, first or last month, or override clears that bill's answers for the same reason, in the live page as well as on restore.
- A restored draft whose `sourceFingerprint` differs from the current fingerprint is **stale**: inputs are kept, derived amounts are recomputed, and a banner says what changed in plain terms (counts of payments, charges, credits, bills, members that differ) and that totals were recomputed. The administrator must pass through Step 4 again before Start, and Start stays disabled while a cleared answer (or any other blocker) is open, with the figures marked pending (frame `#fx-year-end-review-restored-*`).
- When storage is unavailable the draft degrades to in-memory state with a notice ("This draft is not saved on this device"); the page works unchanged.
- **Discard draft** is offered on every step and confirms. The draft is cleared only after confirmed success or explicit discard; a failed or uncertain commit retains it.
- When a next year was created elsewhere during the flow (detected at Start or on reload by the guard), the page says so, names the year, keeps the draft, and offers Discard draft or Open &lt;year&gt;; it never commits.

### Rollover (`BillingYearService.createYear(yearId, { reopenedAdjustmentIds, reconciliation })`)

- **Compatibility boundary.** `reconciliation` omitted (`undefined`) is the legacy call: the same two documents with the same content (the carry, the seeds, the markings, the `YEAR_CARRIED_FORWARD` event), no new records, no new events, now committed through the version-checked transaction of § Versioned year-document writes (the new year `rev: 1`, the source `rev + 1`) instead of a bare `writeBatch`. Its **business outcome** is preserved and asserted by its existing tests; the earlier promise that its `writeBatch` and tests stay byte-for-byte unchanged is withdrawn (decided 2026-11-05, D-A). `reconciliation` present, even `{}` or a "Nothing changed" payload, is the review path. The two are distinguished by `'reconciliation' in options`, not by truthiness.
- Payload: `{ reconciliationId, sourceYearId, sourceFingerprint, trueUps: [...bill-level inputs...], serviceCredits: [...each with its existingCredits answers by adjustment id...], usageCharges: [...], billOverrides: { [billId]: nextYearAmount }, endedBillIds: [...], newBills: [...], confirmations }`; the service rebuilds every record through the pure library (never trusting amounts from the page), re-derives each answer's `seen` and `coverage` from the transaction's fresh read and rejects the payload before any write when an answer is stale, missing, or applies more than the record or the member's remaining share allows.
- Order inside the upcoming reviewed enqueued commit: (1) build the appended source-year arrays (`owedAdjustments` + deferred True-ups + Service Credits + new Usage Charges); (2) finalize the approved deterministic review carry ids in `creditDispositionIds`, then call `buildCarryForwardSummary` over **that** updated state with source/destination labels, `reopenedAdjustmentIds`, review-credit provenance and itemized/unused-credit options; (3) consume the validated plan with `applyCarryForwardToPriorYear` (no id factory), which preserves its finalized ids and carried source markings; (4) `buildNewYearData` consumes the same plan’s itemized seeds with the derived next-year bills; (5) append events; (6) commit both documents atomically. True-ups are therefore never persisted as deferred in the completed reviewed rollover. The ordinary legacy `createYear` path retains its existing generated random ids and uses the same finalize-before-validation boundary.
- Events on the source year: `TRUE_UP_RECORDED` per True-up `{ trueUpId, billId, billName, basis, calc, amount: gross, memberCount, memberIds, reconciliationId }`; `SERVICE_CREDIT_RECORDED` (existing payload plus `basis`, `calc`, `coveredBy: [{ adjustmentId, memberId, appliedAmount }]`, `reconciliationId`); `SERVICE_CREDIT_COVERED { billId, billName, basis, calc, coveredBy, reconciliationId }` for a bill change that was fully covered by existing credits and wrote no record (the audit of the applied ids and amounts); `USAGE_CHARGE_RECORDED` (existing payload plus `reconciliationId`); `YEAR_CARRIED_FORWARD` (existing, plus `reconciliationId` and `itemCount`) when anything carries; always `YEAR_ROLLED_OVER { toYear, reconciliationId, payloadFingerprint, trueUpCount, serviceCreditCount, coveredCount, usageChargeCount, priceChangeCount, endedCount, newBillCount }`. Events on the new year: `YEAR_CREATED_FROM_REVIEW { fromYear, reconciliationId, payloadFingerprint, householdCount, carriedTotal }`; `BILL_PRICE_ROLLED_OVER { billId, billName, previousValue, newValue, billingFrequency, fromYear }` per bill whose next-year price differs from its source price; `BILL_CREATED` per new bill with `source: 'year_end_review'` and `firstMonth`. `BILLING_EVENT_LABELS` gains `TRUE_UP_RECORDED: 'True-up recorded'`, `SERVICE_CREDIT_COVERED: 'Covered by an earlier credit'`, `YEAR_ROLLED_OVER: 'Year rolled over'`, `YEAR_CREATED_FROM_REVIEW: 'Year created'`, `BILL_PRICE_ROLLED_OVER: 'Price set at rollover'`.
- The review path writes the source-year document even when the source year is Closed (the existing rollover exception) and even when nothing carries (the `YEAR_ROLLED_OVER` event), so any concurrent rollover changes the source document.
- Permitted source statuses: `open`, `settling`, `closed`; `archived` is rejected. Service Credits on a Closed source year are permitted under ADR 0009's same-commit disposition rule; because a Closed year's households are all Settled, the review's Service Credits there can only produce a payment-backed surplus, which the carry disposes in the same commit, and the service asserts before commit that no household of a Closed source year reads Outstanding after the appended records. On an Open or Settling source year a Service Credit may lower a household's shortfall or turn it into a carried surplus; it never raises a shortfall (invariant (c) in § Itemized carry).
- `billDeferredCharges` on a household holding a deferred True-up (the standalone-primitive case) bills it like any other deferred Usage Charge; a test confirms it.

### Commit guard and recovery (decided 2026-11-04: a Firestore transaction for the review path)

- The review path commits through `runTransaction`. Inside the callback, in this order: `tx.get` the destination (`users/<uid>/billingYears/<yearId>`) and `tx.get` the source year **before any write**; if the destination exists, throw `NEXT_YEAR_EXISTS` (never overwrite); recompute `computeSourceFingerprint(normalizeYearData(sourceSnapshot))` from the transaction's own read and compare it with the **fixed** `reconciliation.sourceFingerprint` the administrator reviewed; if they differ, throw `STALE_SOURCE` (a new fingerprint is never accepted silently, on any attempt or retry); only then build the source-year payload from the transaction's fresh read plus the appended records and events (never from in-memory state) and `tx.set` both documents. The transaction still runs inside `this._saveQueue.enqueue(...)`, behind any pending save of the active year, and a failure is captured and re-thrown after the queue settles exactly as today. Both documents carry `rev` (§ Versioned year-document writes): the source's expected `rev` is the one the transaction read, the destination is created with `rev: 1`, and the rules check both independently. The legacy path commits through the same version-checked transaction with its own payloads.
- **The callback is free of external side effects**: it mutates no service state, emits no notifications or toasts, allocates no ids (every id is computed before the transaction from the draft), and performs no reads or writes other than its two `get`s and two `set`s, because the SDK may run the callback more than once under contention. State, the draft, and the UI change only after the returned promise settles.
- **Rules and permissions.** The transaction's reads (including a `get` of the absent destination) and its two `set`s are owner writes the rules permit; after the versioned-write rules below are in force they must also carry `rev` as every other writer does, and the reviewed rollover ships only once stage 2 is deployed (§ Versioned year-document writes). The implementation ticket verifies the transaction against the **stage-2** rules with a transaction-shaped rules test (get absent destination, get source, set both with the right `rev`s; a wrong `rev` on either document denied) before claiming it. There is no "no rules change" claim any more.
- `computeSourceFingerprint(state)` is a pure, key-order-independent hash over `status`, `bills` (id, name, amount, billingFrequency, members), `familyMembers` (id, name, linkedMembers), `payments` (id, memberId, amount, type, reversed), `creditAdjustments`, `owedAdjustments`, and `billingEvents.length`. Settings and template fields are excluded. It is computed once when Step 4 is shown and stored on the draft as the reviewed fingerprint; refreshing the preview replaces it only after the administrator sees the new totals.
- **Outcomes.** `NEXT_YEAR_EXISTS` — the destination exists; the service reads its `billingEvents`: a `YEAR_CREATED_FROM_REVIEW` event carrying this draft's `reconciliationId` **and** a `payloadFingerprint` equal to this attempt's (a key-order-independent hash of the canonical reconciliation payload: bill changes and answers, new bills, charges, overrides, confirmations, label) means the operation already completed (switch to it, clear the draft, no second commit). The same `reconciliationId` with a **different** `payloadFingerprint` means the year was created from this draft on another tab with different entries (two tabs on one device restore the same draft and diverge): keep the draft, say so, name what differs in plain counts, offer Open &lt;year&gt; and Discard draft, and never clear a draft whose edits were not the ones committed. No event, or another `reconciliationId`, means it was created elsewhere (keep the draft, show the created-elsewhere state, never commit). `YEAR_ROLLED_OVER` and `YEAR_CREATED_FROM_REVIEW` both carry `payloadFingerprint`; the uncertain-outcome read-back applies the same three-way rule. `STALE_SOURCE` — nothing written; the page refreshes the preview from the current state, shows what changed, and requires Step 4 to be confirmed again before Start is enabled. `COMMIT_FAILED` — a definite failure before or at commit (`aborted` after the SDK's own retries, `failed-precondition`, `permission-denied`, `invalid-argument`, a thrown validation error): nothing written, the draft kept, Retry offered. `COMMIT_UNCERTAIN` — `unavailable`, `deadline-exceeded`, `unknown`, `internal`, a cancelled request, or a non-Firestore error after the commit was sent: the service reads the destination and the source year's events back before any retry and classifies the result as completed (clear the draft, switch), created elsewhere (keep the draft, stop), or not committed (Retry allowed with the same `reconciliationId`, ids, and reviewed fingerprint).
- **Offline.** A Firestore transaction cannot run offline (`tx.get` rejects with `unavailable`, "client is offline"); the page treats it as `COMMIT_UNCERTAIN` only when the commit was sent, otherwise as `COMMIT_FAILED` with the message "You're offline. Nothing was saved; your draft is kept." Retry re-runs the guard and the transaction; it never falls back to a batch or to a write without the preconditions.
- Repeated attempts therefore produce one logically identical rollover: deterministic ids mean a completed commit is recognizable, the destination precondition means it is never overwritten, and the fingerprint means source records are never appended twice.
- **Tests** (`tests/react/lib/BillingYearService.test.js`, with a `runTransaction` mock that can replay the callback, fail the commit, or reject after committing): a stale preview (a payment recorded after the preview → `STALE_SOURCE`, nothing written); two sessions creating the same label (the second gets `NEXT_YEAR_EXISTS` and does not overwrite); two tabs restoring the same draft, diverging, one committing → the other's Start finds the same `reconciliationId` with a different `payloadFingerprint`, keeps its draft and does not report success; a retry after `COMMIT_FAILED` producing exactly the same records and ids; a successful commit whose response is lost (`unavailable` after commit → read-back finds `YEAR_CREATED_FROM_REVIEW` with the reconciliation id → completed, no second commit); the callback replayed by the SDK producing no duplicate records or side effects; a definite failure leaving no destination document and no source change; and the legacy call committing through the same version-checked transaction once, with its documents unchanged apart from `rev`.
- **Writers of `users/<uid>/billingYears/<yearId>` and what the transaction does and does not protect.** The client writes the year document from four places: `save()` (a full-document `setDoc` without merge of the active year, built from in-memory state that was loaded once by `getDoc`; nothing subscribes with `onSnapshot`), `setYearStatus()` (a merge `setDoc` of `status` and the **whole** in-memory `billingEvents` array), the legacy `createYear()` (`writeBatch`: `set` of the new year and, when anything carries, `set` of the source from memory, after a duplicate check against the in-memory years list), and `loadData()` (the first year's `setDoc` when none exists). The Cloud Functions write `publicShares`, `shareTokens`, `disputes`, `mailQueue` and rate-limit documents, never the year document. A tab running a build deployed before this feature keeps running it until reload (`UpdateToast` polls `version.json` every 60 s and shows a banner; it does not reload). The rules allow every one of these writes on the owner condition alone. **Enforceable today:** the reviewed path never overwrites an existing next year and never commits over a source that changed after the preview, against **any** writer, because the transaction's own reads decide (reviewed-versus-reviewed yields exactly one commit; a stale preview yields `STALE_SOURCE`). **Not enforceable today:** (1) *legacy-versus-reviewed*: a pre-feature tab whose years list predates the new year passes its duplicate check and its `writeBatch` overwrites the new year with a legacy-shaped document and, if anything carried, the source with its stale arrays (the True-ups, review Service Credits, `carry_forward` records and rollover events vanish from the source while the new year's seeds, if they survive, still hold the money); (2) *stale-save-after-rollover*: any tab (old or new build) that loaded the source before the rollover and then saves (recording a payment is enough) replaces the source with its pre-rollover arrays, with the same loss, and a stale `setYearStatus` replaces the events (the successor guard's second test, the higher numeric label, still blocks a second review). No client code can stop a pre-feature tab, and the current rules cannot tell a stale write from a fresh one. On its own the transaction therefore gives no universal no-overwrite guarantee. **Decided 2026-11-05 (D-A, option 2):** every writer of the year document becomes version-checked and Firestore rules enforce the version (§ Versioned year-document writes), staged so that the rules are in force before the reviewed rollover ships. After stage 2 the two sequences above are refused by the rules for every build, and the commit-guard tests assert the refusal; until stage 2 lands, the tests assert the current behavior so the gap is visible, not claimed fixed.

### Versioned year-document writes (decided 2026-11-05: D-A, option 2)

- **Contract.** Every write to `users/<uid>/billingYears/<yearId>` carries an integer `rev` and is version-checked twice: by the client, which writes only through a `runTransaction` that reads the document, compares the stored `rev` with the `rev` it loaded (`expectedRev`) and writes `rev = expectedRev + 1`; and by the Firestore rules below, which require `create` to carry `rev == 1` and `update` to carry `rev == resource.data.rev + 1`. The writers covered: `save()` (the full document), `setYearStatus()` (which stops being a direct merge write and becomes a full-document write of the same payload through the queue), the legacy `createYear` (both documents), the reviewed rollover (both documents, in addition to its destination-exists and fixed-fingerprint checks, which stay), and the first-run creation in `loadData()`. All of them run through the `SaveQueue`, so a status change is serialized behind a pending save and rapid same-tab edits commit in order; the service's in-memory `rev` advances only when a commit resolves, so each queued write expects the right value. `rev` rides `buildSavePayload`'s allowlist (ADR 0008 amended) and `normalizeYearData` (`year.rev`, `undefined` for an unversioned document). `save:success` is emitted only after the transaction resolves.
- **Stale write outcome.** When the transaction's read shows a `rev` other than `expectedRev` (or, for an unversioned document, a different fingerprint, below), nothing is written; the service raises `STALE_DOCUMENT`, the queue stops accepting writes for that year (every later enqueue rejects with the same outcome), and the app enters a read-only **Reload required** state: a blocking dialog says the year changed in another tab or device, names the time of the tab's **last successful save** (the baseline advances every time a transaction resolves; the loaded snapshot is only the first baseline, so a payment saved after the load is never listed as unsaved), lists the unsaved changes from the **unsaved payload**, not from events alone: the billing events appended since that baseline (label, amount, member, time) plus a structural diff of the payload against the baseline for changes that emit no event (`addMember`, member edits and removals, bill edits, `updateSettings`: "Member added: Priya Natarajan", "Invoice template changed"), and offers **Copy details** (the summary and the full unsaved payload as JSON) and **Reload**. A `permission-denied` from the rules (a stale client against enforced rules, or any write the rules refuse) produces the same state. Nothing is merged automatically: the app never applies a fresh `rev` to stale data, and the saving indicator shows *Not saved*, never a success tick, for a write that did not commit. The unsaved state is also kept on the device (`localStorage` key `ffb.unsaved.v1.<uid>.<yearId>`, try/catch, the summary plus the serialized payload) so it survives the reload; after the reload the Dashboard shows *You had unsaved changes from <time>* with the same list and Copy details until dismissed. **When the on-device write fails** (storage blocked, quota exceeded, private mode) the dialog says so ("This list could not be kept on this device—copy it before you reload"), Copy details stays available, and Reload is enabled only after Copy details was used or the administrator confirms "Reload without copying"; tests cover unavailable and quota-exceeded storage. Frame `#fx-dashboard-stale-*`.
- **Lost response and offline.** A transaction that fails before commit leaves `expectedRev` unchanged and the next save retries with the same expectation. When the outcome is uncertain (`unavailable`, `deadline-exceeded`, `unknown`, `internal`, a dropped connection after the commit was sent), the next transaction's read settles it against two things the service kept for the attempt: `expectedRev` and `writeId`, a client-generated UUID allocated per attempt and written into the document as `lastWriteId` (allowlisted alongside `rev`). A **write identity, not a payload fingerprint**, is used because every write carries server timestamps (`updatedAt`, sometimes `createdAt`) that resolve on the server, so a literal comparison of the sent payload with the stored document would read a successful save as a conflict. Stored `rev == expectedRev + 1` **and** stored `lastWriteId == writeId` means the write committed (adopt the `rev`, advance the baseline, continue); stored `rev == expectedRev` means it did not (retry with a new `writeId`); anything else is `STALE_DOCUMENT`. **The first versioned write of an unversioned document** has no `expectedRev`: committed means stored `rev == 1` and stored `lastWriteId == writeId`; a stored `rev == 1` with another `lastWriteId`, or any other `rev`, is `STALE_DOCUMENT`; a document still without `rev` whose fingerprint equals the loaded snapshot's means not committed (retry). Every fingerprint in this spec (`computeDocumentFingerprint`, `computeSourceFingerprint`) excludes `createdAt`, `updatedAt`, `lastWriteId` and `rev`. A test writes with a server timestamp, lets it resolve, drops the response, and asserts the save is adopted. A test drops the response to the migration write and asserts the save is adopted without `STALE_DOCUMENT`. A transaction cannot run offline; the save is reported as *Not saved* with the offline message and retried on the next save, never downgraded to a plain write.
- **Rules (narrow; `firestore.rules`, the one change this epic makes to it).** Reads are unchanged (owner only). Writes:

  ```text
  match /billingYears/{yearId} {
    function isOwner() { return request.auth != null && request.auth.uid == userId; }
    function reqRev() { return 'rev' in request.resource.data && request.resource.data.rev is int; }
    function noRev() { return !('rev' in request.resource.data); }                              // absent, not merely malformed
    function hasRev() { return 'rev' in resource.data && resource.data.rev is int; }

    function sameCreatedAt() { return 'createdAt' in resource.data && request.resource.data.createdAt == resource.data.createdAt; }  // present AND unchanged
    allow read: if isOwner();
    allow create: if isOwner() && ((reqRev() && request.resource.data.rev == 1)
                                || noRev());                                                  // STAGE 1 ONLY: an unversioned create (a pre-feature tab's first-run or legacy year creation); removed at stage 2
    allow update: if isOwner() && (
         (reqRev() && hasRev() && request.resource.data.rev == resource.data.rev + 1)      // a versioned write advances by exactly one
      || (reqRev() && storedNoRev() && request.resource.data.rev == 1 && sameCreatedAt())  // the first versioned write of an unversioned document
      || (noRev() && storedNoRev()));                                                       // STAGE 1 ONLY: an unversioned write onto an unversioned document (old tabs during the rollout); removed at stage 2
    allow delete: if false;
    match /disputes/{disputeId} { allow read, write: if isOwner(); }                         // unchanged
  }
  ```

  `sameCreatedAt()` is what keeps a create-shaped rollover payload (whose `createdAt` is the server time) from overwriting an existing *unversioned* year through the migration branch; a genuine first versioned save carries the stored `createdAt`. It requires the stored `createdAt` to be **present**: the rules cannot evaluate the client's fingerprint, so a stored document without `createdAt` has no server-checkable anchor and cannot be migrated through this branch at all. Every creation and save path writes `createdAt` (`buildInitialYearData`, `buildNewYearData` and `save()` all set it), so no such document is expected; #615 verifies that by query before stage 1 and, if any exists, backfills `createdAt` once through the Admin SDK (set only where absent) before the rules deploy. Rules tests: a migration write with a changed `createdAt` is denied; a migration write onto a document without `createdAt` is denied. The stage-1 create allowance applies to `create` only (an absent document), so it never lets a pre-feature tab overwrite an existing year of either kind. A **malformed `rev`** (present but not an integer, such as `"1"`, `1.5` or `null`) matches neither `reqRev()` nor `noRev()` and is denied at every stage; a stored document whose `rev` is present but not an integer matches no update branch either — the migration and stage-1 branches require the stored field to be **absent** (`storedNoRev()`), not merely non-integer — so it fails closed until repaired by an administrator-run migration, never silently accepted as unversioned at either stage. A `writeBatch` or transaction that touches two year documents fails as a whole when either document fails its check. **Delete:** no client or server path deletes a year document, and none is planned; the rule denies it explicitly, so a year can only be archived. The disputes subcollection and every other collection are untouched.
- **Migration of unversioned documents.** Existing documents have no `rev`. A document becomes versioned by its first version-checked write, which sets `rev: 1` and may proceed only when the transaction's read equals the loaded snapshot by **document fingerprint** (`computeDocumentFingerprint(normalizeYearData(doc))`: a key-order-independent hash of every allowlisted field, status and label, **excluding** `createdAt`, `updatedAt`, `lastWriteId` and `rev`, because server timestamps resolve on the server and a sent sentinel never equals a stored value; `updatedAt` alone could not serve anyway, since the current `setYearStatus` does not bump it). A mismatch means an unversioned writer changed the document after the load: `STALE_DOCUMENT`, reload. Treating a missing `rev` as zero is explicitly not the rule. No migration script is needed: after stage 2 every remaining unversioned document is versioned by its owner's first write under the same check, and until then an old tab can only touch a still-unversioned document, which the fingerprint check catches.
- **Staged rollout, rules before the reviewed rollover.** *Stage 1* (two tickets, rules first): deploy the rules above, which accept versioned writes and still accept, from pre-feature tabs, an unversioned create (first-run and legacy year creation) and an unversioned update onto an unversioned document; then ship the client that writes `rev` through transactions with the stale-write recovery. **Protection, and the old-tab loss window, begin per document at its first versioned write, which happens at stage 1:** from that write on, an old tab's unversioned save to that document is refused, and the old build's `SaveQueue` only logs the refusal and continues. So the window the owner acknowledges opens with the stage-1 client deploy, for any document the new client has written, and closes as old tabs reload (the acknowledgment is on #614 for the client deploy and on #616 for enforcement). Until a document's first versioned write, stage 1 does **not** protect it: an unversioned legacy batch onto an existing unversioned destination is accepted, as is an unversioned update. *Stage 2*: remove both stage-1 allowances so every write must carry `rev`, after the stage-1 client has been live for at least one release cycle with the update banner (`UpdateToast`, 60-second poll) and the stage-1 rules tests have been green on `main`; a tab running the **new** client that loaded before stage 2 is refused on its first write (`permission-denied`) and recovers through the same Reload required state. **A pre-feature tab cannot:** its `SaveQueue` logs `save:error` and keeps going, with no dialog, no read-only state and no on-device copy, and nothing in the new build or the rules can change a build that is already running. That window is an **accepted limitation of stage 2, acknowledged by the owner in #616 before it deploys**, bounded by the mitigations: stage 2 waits for the stage-1 client to have been live for a full release cycle with the update banner (60-second poll, which the owner may shorten for that deploy), the stage-1 allowances keep pre-feature tabs working until then, and a pre-feature tab's edits are lost only if it is used for writes after the stage-2 deploy without reloading. The reviewed rollover (#606, #607, #609) ships only after stage 2 is in force: #607 is natively blocked on the stage-2 ticket.
- **Legacy `createYear`.** The business outcome is unchanged (same two documents, same carry, seeds, markings and event, apart from `rev`), but it commits through the version-checked transaction inside the queue (source at `expectedRev`, destination created with `rev: 1`, which the rules also refuse when the destination exists). The promise that its `writeBatch` and tests stay byte-for-byte unchanged is withdrawn; its tests are updated for `rev` and the transaction mock and keep every business assertion.
- **Limitations the rules cannot cover (stated, not claimed away).** (1) Admin SDK writers bypass rules. No Cloud Function writes a year document today; `tests/functions.test.js` gains a source guard asserting none does, and any future server writer must implement the same read-and-increment contract inside a transaction. (2) The rules verify `rev == stored + 1` at commit time, not that the writer read the document; a writer that guessed the current `rev` could still overwrite. Every writer in this repository reads first, and the guard above covers the server. (3) The rules cannot compare content, so the unversioned-document fingerprint check is client-side only and therefore not enforced against old tabs during stage 1; stage 2 closes that. (4) The `users/<uid>` document's `activeBillingYear` pointer is not versioned (last writer wins is acceptable for a pointer). (5) Rules cannot tell the reviewed rollover from the legacy one; both are held to the same checks.
- **Tests.** Rules (`tests/rules/firestore.rules.test.js`): a versioned write at `stored + 1` succeeds; the same `rev` or `+ 2` fails; `create` with `rev: 1` on an absent document succeeds and on an existing versioned document fails; a create-shaped payload onto an unversioned document fails on `createdAt`; an unversioned write onto a versioned document fails; an unversioned write onto an unversioned document succeeds at stage 1 and fails at stage 2; an unversioned create (first-run `setDoc`, legacy `createYear` destination) succeeds at stage 1 and fails at stage 2 (the suite runs both rule files); a malformed `rev` (`"1"`, `1.5`, `null`) is denied on create and update at both stages; an update onto a stored document with a malformed `rev` is denied at both stages, whether the request carries `rev: 1`, the next integer, or no `rev` at all; a batch holding one stale document fails entirely; two concurrent transactions on the same document leave exactly one committed; `delete` is denied; the disputes rules are unchanged. Service (`tests/react/lib/BillingYearService.test.js`, `tests/react/lib/SaveQueue.test.js`): rapid same-tab edits commit in order with consecutive `rev`s; a status change waits behind a pending save; two sessions saving the same year leave one `STALE_DOCUMENT` with no write; the first-run race (two tabs creating the same first year) leaves one document; a lost response is classified by the next read through `lastWriteId`, including for the first versioned write of an unversioned document and with a resolved server timestamp in the stored document; offline and `permission-denied` produce the Reload required state with the unsaved list persisted; the legacy `createYear` produces the same documents as before apart from `rev`; the reviewed rollover carries `rev` on both documents and keeps its destination-exists and fingerprint checks. Old tabs are simulated by writing without `rev` against each rules stage.

### Member-facing surfaces

- `buildPublicShareData` includes `carriedItems: { fromYear, items: [...member-safe items...], total, remainingCredit }` under `summary:read` when the household's seeds carry items, where two different numbers are kept apart: `notApplied = max(0, −(billsSubtotal − serviceCreditTotal + total))` is the household's **floor remainder** (the "Not applied this year" row; it includes any Service Credit excess, which the rollover forfeits), and `remainingCredit` is the **unused incoming credit**, computed **from the net `total` exactly as the rollover computes `U`** (§ Itemized carry): `I = max(0, −total)`, `Q = max(0, total)`, `A = max(0, billsSubtotal − serviceCreditTotal + Q)`, `remainingCredit = max(0, I − A)`. It is the only number the "$Y remains" sentence may promise, and it never exceeds `notApplied`. The seed's charge/credit split (§ Household preview) is display only and **never enters this formula**: a seed is one net number, its charge and credit net against each other before anything else, and a Service Credit excess, which the rollover forfeits, can never absorb a carried charge so that a carried credit appears to remain (a +$10.00 charge and −$10.00 credit under $100.00 of bills and $200.00 of Service Credits carry nothing and show nothing remaining; fixture 10n). `specs/sharing.md` defines both fields the same way; fixtures 10l and 10n (member-safe fields only: `memberId`, `memberName`, `kind`, `basis`, `billName`, `description`, `amount`, `detail[]` with the same fields; never ids of other households). `ShareView` renders the section after the household total reconciliation and before the summary figures. The carried amount in the summary figures is likewise a link to the section; nothing says "see below".
- `getInvoiceSummaryContext` exposes `carriedItems` and `buildFullInvoiceText` renders a CARRIED FROM 2026 block between the bill tables and the payment summary; the Invoicing preview and the email dialog's Full invoice variant show the same lines.
- The Cloud Function `resolveShareToken` builds `carriedItems` through a `functions/billing.js` mirror of the client builder, includes it in the response and in the self-healed `publicShares` document (reconciled with `FieldValue.delete()` when absent, like `serviceCredits`), and an explicit cache↔function parity test asserts the two builders produce identical output. ### Future Notice warning at close and rollover (#648; Proposed ADR)

The [owner decision](https://github.com/nathanjohnpayne/friends-and-family-billing/pull/649#issuecomment-6716004056)
accepts the [ADR 0012](../docs/adr/0012-charge-notice-recovery.md) contract choices
and four amendments. ADR 0012 remains Proposed pending review and merge of the
final amended head. This is future-contract text, not runtime conformance, code
approval, a #614 old-tab acknowledgment or deployment/live-report authorization.

- Before confirming close or rollover, use the shared R4 warning read/API to list
  all Notice intents not `sent`, with count, Notice/household identities and
  delivery status: blocked/not-addressable, pending, processing, unknown and
  proven pre-dispatch failure. A failed read shows **Delivery status unavailable**
  as a warning, not an empty list.
- The administrator may knowingly proceed. Delivery status adds no financial
  integrity hold, settlement lock, close/rollover refusal or readiness gate.
  Existing exact-source holds, outstanding-money and lifecycle gates remain.
- Explain that after close the Notices are historical only: no new dispatch
  claim or recovery authorization. Unclaimed unsent Notices will never be sent
  later. A send already authorized by a successful claim before close may finish
  externally; the UI must not promise cancellation of that send.
- R4 integrates the existing close/rollover paths. Future #606/#607 review flows
  retain the same warning contract/API without a reverse R4 dependency on those
  tickets. The warning creates no year-document writer and no change to version
  checks, atomic rollover, draft preservation or source/destination safeguards.
- The review's `buildUsageChargeRecord` and existing True-up `splitCents` writer
  retain checked whole-cent outputs and tripwire coverage. This is evidence about
  these admitted writer outputs, not a claim about every stored legacy record;
  malformed/sub-cent source admission remains governed by ADR 0010.

Future validation includes each not-sent state and read failure; knowingly
proceeding without new financial gates; blocked/new post-close claims and an
already claimed send completing; shared warning preservation in the reviewed
rollover; and writer tripwires. No implementation/test patch is part of this
amendment.

Uncommunicated Notices never remove their billed source rows or alter money.
These warnings define no new billed-charge auto-carry rule: existing financial
authority and frozen owed/settlement/carry formulas remain decisive.
