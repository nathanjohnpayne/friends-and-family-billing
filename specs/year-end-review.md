# Year-End Review

Covers the guided page through which a new billing year is created: the closing year's bill changes (True-ups and Service Credits), subscriptions that started mid-year, member charges, the per-household preview, the atomic rollover that appends the review's records to the closing year and seeds the new year with an itemized carry, the local draft, the next-year guard, and the member-facing "Carried from &lt;year&gt;" section. Decisions: [ADR 0009](../docs/adr/0009-year-end-review-true-ups-and-itemized-carry.md). Wireframes: `plans/ffb-wireframes.html` (`#fx-year-end-…`, `#fx-settings-populated-…`, `#fx-dashboard-yearend-…`, `#fx-share-carried-…`, `#fx-invoicing-preview-carried-…`, `#fx-dlg-email-invoice-full-carried-…`, `#fx-dlg-bill-history-rollover-…`).

Out of scope for v1: standalone mid-year price changes or late starts (the pure builders stay callable), mid-year membership changes on a bill, frequency changes as part of a price change, day-level proration or non-calendar years, new UI for off-cycle billing True-ups, effective-dated pricing or price history on the bill model.

## Acceptance Criteria

### Vocabulary and units

- Administrator-facing copy (the review page, billing events, the bill history dialog) may say True-up. Member-facing copy (share page, invoice preview, invoice email, text invoice) never says True-up, carry_opening, reconciliation, or adjustment; the approved section heading is **Carried from &lt;year&gt;** and every line says what happened and when (see [Member-facing copy](#member-facing-copy)).
- **Money is stored in dollars as JavaScript numbers rounded to cents**, the existing persisted convention (`amount: shareCents / 100`). Arithmetic is done in integer cents and converted once: `toCents(x) = Math.round((x + Number.EPSILON) * 100)`, the convention `recordUsageCharge` and `recordServiceCredit` already use.
- **Signs.** An `owedAdjustments[]` record's `amount` is a positive magnitude; `kind` gives the direction (`usage_charge` raises owed, `service_credit` lowers it). A `carry_opening` seed's `amount` is signed (a carried charge positive, a carried credit negative), as today. `items[].amount` and `detail[].amount` are signed the same way (charge `+`, credit `−`). Preview and display totals are signed; the UI renders `+$12.00` and `−$26.00`.
- `calc` stores the amounts the administrator entered in the bill's own frequency plus the derived monthly figures: `{ frequency: 'monthly' | 'annual', fromAmount, toAmount, fromMonthly, toMonthly, firstMonth, lastMonth, months, computed, gross, overridden }`. `fromMonthly`/`toMonthly` are `amount` for a monthly bill and `amount / 12` **unrounded** for an annual bill; `firstMonth` / `lastMonth` are `YYYY-MM` strings inside the source year (`lastMonth` only for `basis: 'ended'`, `fromAmount`/`fromMonthly` null for `basis: 'late_start'`); `months` is the count the formula used; `computed` is the formula's result in dollars; `gross` is the amount actually recorded (equal to `computed` unless `overridden: true`); the per-member records sum exactly to `gross`.

### True-up and Service Credit math (`src/lib/year-end-review.js`, pure)

- `monthsFromFirst(firstMonth)` returns `13 − month(firstMonth)` (July → 6, January → 12, December → 1). `monthsAfterLast(lastMonth)` returns `12 − month(lastMonth)` (August → 4, December → 0). Both reject a month outside the source year.
- **Price change.** `grossCents = toCents((toMonthly − fromMonthly) × months)` with `months = monthsFromFirst(firstMonth)`. A positive result is a True-up (`kind: 'usage_charge'`, `basis: 'price_change'`); a negative result is a Service Credit (`kind: 'service_credit'`, `basis: 'price_change'`) of the absolute value. For an annual bill `toMonthly − fromMonthly = (toAmount − fromAmount) / 12`, multiplied by `months` **before** rounding: the annual-increase fixture is $16.67, not $16.70.
- **Late start.** `grossCents = toCents(toMonthly × months)` with `months = monthsFromFirst(firstMonth)`; a True-up with `basis: 'late_start'`.
- **Ended.** `grossCents = toCents(fromMonthly × monthsAfterLast(lastMonth))`; a Service Credit with `basis: 'ended'`. The last month of service is included in what was used; the credit covers the following month through December.
- **Existing Service Credits on the bill (matching is an owner decision, see § Decisions pending).** A bill marked Ended or Price changed (downward) may already carry active Service Credits recorded during the year from the Bills tab or a Review Request resolution (`recordServiceCredit`: `billId`, `memberId`, `amount`, free-text `reason`, `incurredDate`, `status: 'active'`; no period, no basis). The stored data therefore cannot tell whether an earlier credit covers the same months as this change, a different period, a different reason, or only one member, so the row never nets silently. The recommended design (pending the decision) is: the row lists every active Service Credit on the bill (date, reason, members, amount) under the heading "Credits already on this bill in 2026", each with a two-way choice **Covers this change** / **Unrelated** and no default; Next is disabled until every listed credit has a choice ("Say whether the $48.00 credit from Sep 2 covers this change."). Netting is **per member**: `net_m = max(0, share_m − Σ matched credit records of member m)`, the recorded gross is `Σ net_m`, one record per member with `net_m > 0`, and the formula shows the subtraction ("$48.00 computed − $48.00 already credited = $0.00"). A member-specific earlier credit reduces only that member's share; an unrelated earlier credit changes nothing. The row never voids or edits an existing credit. When every member nets to zero the row says what happens instead of recording: "Already fully credited—nothing more to record; the bill is left out of 2027" (Ended) or "…; 2027 uses $24.00" (Price changed).
- **Split.** `splitCents(totalCents, memberIds)` is one shared helper, extracted from `recordServiceCredit` with its exact behavior: `base = Math.floor(totalCents / n)`, every member but the last gets `base`, the last gets `totalCents − base × (n − 1)`, so the shares sum exactly to `totalCents`. `recordServiceCredit` calls it and its existing tests pass unchanged.
- **Override.** The administrator may replace the computed gross amount; the record keeps `calc.computed`, sets `calc.gross` to the override and `calc.overridden: true`, and the split (and any per-member netting against matched existing credits) is of the override. An override must be a positive amount in the same direction as the formula: a True-up cannot be overridden into a credit (use a Service Credit row instead) and **there is no override of 0**. A recorded amount of 0 is never typed; it arises only from the two zero cases below.
- **Zero cases are explicit, and the outcome depends on the change, not on the amount.** Exactly two situations record nothing: `months = 0` (a bill ended in December) and every member netting to zero against matched existing credits. In both, an **Ended** bill is still left out of the new year and a **Price changed** bill still appears in the new year at its new price; the row states which ("Nothing more to record; the bill is left out of 2027" / "Nothing more to record; 2027 uses $24.00"). A price change whose new price equals the current price is a validation error ("No change—leave the bill Unchanged or enter a different price"), never a zero-amount record. An override of 0 or less is a validation error. A late-start bill with no members is a validation error. A Service Credit on a bill whose members are empty is impossible by construction (the bill's members are the split).
- **Formula strings** shown beside every computed amount: price change `($198.00 − $180.00) × 6 months = $108.00 ÷ 9 members = $12.00 each`; annual `($159.00 − $139.00) ÷ 12 × 10 months = $16.67 ÷ 3 members = $5.55 · $5.55 · $5.57`; late start `$9.99 × 4 months = $39.96 ÷ 3 members = $13.32 each`; ended `$12.00 × 4 months (Sep–Dec) = $48.00 ÷ 3 members = $16.00 each`. An overridden row shows `computed $108.00 → entered $100.00`.

### Record shapes

- **True-up records** (one per member of the bill): `{ id: 'oadj_tu_<sourceYearId>_<trueUpId>_<memberId>', trueUpId, memberId, billId, billName, kind: 'usage_charge', basis: 'price_change' | 'late_start', amount, description, incurredDate, calc, status, createdAt, reconciliationId }`. `trueUpId` is `tu_<sourceYearId>_<billId>` for a price change and `tu_<sourceYearId>_new_<newBillId>` for a late start. `description` is the member-facing sentence (below). `incurredDate` is the first day of `firstMonth`. `status` is `'deferred'` in the in-memory reconciliation and `'carried_forward'` in the first persisted state (ADR 0009 § 3). A Usage Charge record with no `basis` is treated as `basis: 'member'` everywhere.
- **Review Service Credit records**: the existing `recordServiceCredit` shape plus `basis: 'price_change' | 'ended'`, `calc`, `reconciliationId`, `id: 'oadj_sc_<sourceYearId>_<basis>_<billId>_<memberId>'`, `reason` set to the member-facing sentence, `status: 'active'`.
- **Member charges** added in Step 3: the existing `recordUsageCharge` shape (`basis` absent) with `id: 'oadj_uc_<sourceYearId>_<rowId>'` and `reconciliationId`, where `rowId` is allocated once when the row is created in the draft and stored on it. Existing deferred charges shown in Step 3 are reused by id and never re-recorded.
- **New bills** in Step 2 allocate `billId` once with `generateUniqueBillId(existingIds)` when the row is created and store it on the draft; the True-up's `billId` and the new year's bill use that id. On draft restoration a `billId` that collides with a current source-year bill is reallocated and the row is flagged for re-check.
- **The carried credit** record on the source year uses the deterministic id `cadj_carry_<nextYearLabel>_<primaryMemberId>` (the `applyCarryForwardToPriorYear` default factory), so the review path never passes a random `idFactory`.
- **New persisted fields** on `owedAdjustments[]` records (`basis`, `calc`, `trueUpId`, `billName`, `reconciliationId`) and on `carry_opening` seeds (`items[]`) ride the verbatim array round-trip of `buildSavePayload`; no allowlist change (ADR 0008). Completion evidence lives in `billingEvents[]`, also already in the allowlist.
- No change to `firestore.rules` or `storage.rules`.

### Itemized carry (`src/lib/billing-year.js`)

- `buildCarryOpeningItems(household, sourceYear)` builds, from the ids `buildCarryForward` already returns for a household (`deferredChargeIds`, `credit`, `creditAdjustmentIds`), the seed's `items[]`: one item per carried deferred Usage Charge `{ sourceId: <charge id>, kind: 'usage_charge', basis, memberId, billName, description, amount: +charge.amount, incurredDate, calc }` (True-ups included, `basis: 'price_change' | 'late_start'`; member charges `basis: 'member'`), and, when the household carries a Credit, exactly one `{ sourceId: 'cadj_carry_<nextYearLabel>_<primaryMemberId>', kind: 'credit', basis: 'credit', memberId: <primary>, billName: null, description: 'Your household's <year> credit', amount: −(credit + unusedOpeningCredit), detail: [...] }` (when only the unused incoming credit carries, `sourceId` is the source seed's id, since no `carry_forward` record exists). `src/lib/calculations.js` is unchanged.
- `detail[]` provenance: `buildCarryForward` returns no Service Credit ids, so the review path passes its own Service Credit records to `buildCarryOpeningItems` through `ctx.reviewServiceCredits` (the records built for this `reconciliationId`, before they are appended), and the helper filters them by the household's member ids. Service Credits recorded earlier in the year (from the Bills tab or a Review Request) are never listed in `detail[]`; they are part of the remainder line. The unused incoming credit comes from the household entry's `unusedOpeningCredit`. The legacy path passes none and produces no detail.
- `detail[]` on the credit item is the household credit's reconciliation: the review's Service Credits for the household's members `{ sourceId, basis, memberId, billName, description, amount: −share }`; the unused incoming credit line `{ kind: 'unused', description: 'Unused <year> credit', amount: −U }` when `U > 0` (below); and one closing line when the running sum still differs from the item: `{ kind: 'remainder', description: 'Other <year> overpayment', amount: −(credit − Σ Service Credits) }` when the payment-backed credit exceeds the review's Service Credits, or `{ kind: 'applied', description: 'Part applied to your <year> balance', amount: +(Σ Service Credits − credit) }` when the Service Credits exceed it. **`Σ detail[].amount` equals the credit item's `amount`**; detail is never added to the seed, to `items[]`, or to any total (the item is).
- **Invariants (property-tested over generated fixtures: random households, payments, refunds, incoming seeds, charges, credits, True-ups, Service Credits).** (a) For every seed, `Σ items[].amount` equals the seed's signed `amount` within `CREDIT_EPSILON`, and `Σ detail[].amount` equals the credit item's `amount`. (b) **Conservation:** for every household, `seed.amount = deferredChargeTotal − (credit + U)` where `credit` is the household's `getHouseholdFinancials` credit in the source year after the review's records and `U` is its unused incoming credit; nothing else enters or leaves. (c) **Old-year settlement:** after the commit, every household in the source year has `credit = 0`, and its shortfall (`owed − netContribution`) is exactly 0 when it carried a payment-backed credit and **unchanged** otherwise; the carry never creates debt in the source year. (d) Over a chain of rollovers with no payments and no bills, the carried credit is identical in every year (fixture 10f).
- Each item snapshots `billName` and `description` at rollover; renderers never look up the new year's bill names or prices for carried items.
- `buildNewYearData(..., carry, fromYearLabel)` writes `items` on each seed when the carry supplies them; a carry without items (the legacy call) seeds exactly as today. Seeds written before this change have no `items`. **Zero net:** when a household's deferred charges and credit cancel (`buildCarryForward` keeps the household, the source year marks the charge `carried_forward` and writes the `carry_forward` record, and `openingBalance` is within `CREDIT_EPSILON` of 0), the review path still writes the seed with `amount: 0` and the household's `items[]`, so the carried lines are explained and invariant (a) holds; the legacy call skips a zero balance as today. Fixture 10j.
- Attribution: a True-up or member charge item is attributed to its `memberId` (a linked member's item shows under that member within the household); the credit item is attributed to the household, never to one member as though it were that member's charge.
- **Incoming credit is money; a Service Credit is not.** `getHouseholdFinancials` floors the bill-derived owed at 0 (`owed = max(0, grossOwed − serviceCreditTotal + priorOpeningBalance) + billedChargeTotal`) and `credit = max(0, netContribution − owed)`. Two different things disappear in that floor and must be treated differently. **An over-large Service Credit** (credits exceeding the bills) is a reduction of charges with no money behind it: the existing rule forfeits the excess ("neither an over-large credit nor a carried credit can make the bills owe the member money"), nothing is carried for it, and the review keeps that rule (fixture 10e). **An incoming credit** (a negative `carry_opening` seed) is money the household paid in an earlier year, disposed there by a `carry_forward` record; when it exceeds the year's charges the floor hides the remainder, `credit` reads 0, and `buildCarryForward` would drop it at the next rollover (the #322 edge case). The review path preserves it. Definitions, in cents, from the values `getHouseholdFinancials` returns for the household: `I = max(0, −priorOpeningBalance)` (incoming credit), `Q = max(0, priorOpeningBalance)` (incoming charge), `A = max(0, grossOwed − serviceCreditTotal + Q)` (charges after Service Credits, the existing floor), **`U = max(0, I − A)`** (unused incoming credit). The household's ledger balance is `netContribution + I − A − billedChargeTotal = (netContribution − owed) + U`, so `U` is exactly the part of the money the floor hides. `buildCarryForwardSummary(..., { carryUnusedOpeningCredit: true })` (review path only; the legacy call keeps its carry unchanged) adds, for every household with `U > CREDIT_EPSILON` (including households `buildCarryForward` omitted because they had no credit and no deferred charge), `unusedOpeningCredit: U` and `openingSeedIds` to the household entry and subtracts `U` from its `openingBalance`; `credit` is **not** changed, so `applyCarryForwardToPriorYear` still writes a `carry_forward` record only for the payment-backed credit. **The unused incoming credit is carried without a disposition record**: a `carry_forward` record subtracts from `netContribution`, and `U` was never in `netContribution`, so disposing it that way would turn the source year's Settled household into one Outstanding by `U` (the finding's second example). Instead `applyCarryForwardToPriorYear` stamps `carriedForwardTo: <nextYearLabel>` on the household's source `carry_opening` seeds (status stays `carried_in`; `getHouseholdOpeningBalance` reads only `amount` and `status !== 'voided'`, so the source year's owed, credit and status are identical before and after). `buildCarryOpeningItems` makes the credit item `amount: −(credit + U)` with the `unused` detail line. `src/lib/calculations.js` is unchanged. Because `credit` is gated on `netContribution`, the Record Refund path never sees `U`: an unused incoming credit can only carry, never be refunded in the year it was unused (a limitation of the frozen owed formula, recorded in ADR 0009). Billed Usage Charges are never offset by it (#320): a household with an unpaid billed charge and an unused incoming credit carries `U` and keeps its shortfall in the source year (fixture 10h). Member-facing surfaces show a $0.00 total for a household whose carried credit exceeds its bills, with the line "Your <year> credit of $X covers this year; $Y remains."
- Fixtures 9 and 10 (§ Worked examples) are the unit fixtures for this rule: the excess-credit chain, and the conservation set (unpaid, partially paid, overpaid, already refunded, over-large Service Credit, no bills, partly used incoming credit, an unpaid billed charge, and an incoming credit with a review Service Credit).

### Next-year bills

- `deriveNextYearBills(sourceBills, reviewBills, newBills)` returns the new year's `bills[]`: every source bill not marked Ended, with `amount` set to its next-year price (defaulting to the new price when the price changed, otherwise the current price; always editable), the same `billingFrequency`, members, logo, and website; plus each new subscription as a bill with the draft's `billId`, name, amount, frequency, website, and members. Ended bills are absent.
- The source year's `bills[]` is never changed: no amount edits, no late-start bills added.
- A next-year price must be a positive number in the bill's frequency; an increase announced for January is entered here without a True-up.

### Household preview (Step 4)

- `buildHouseholdPreview(...)` returns, per household (primary plus linked members, ADR 0001): `{ primaryMemberId, members: [{ memberId, sourceBillTotal, nextBillTotal }], sourceBillTotal, nextBillTotal, subscriptionDelta, items: [...], carriedTotal, firstInvoiceTotal, remainingCredit }` where `sourceBillTotal` is the household's **bill-derived annual total in the closing year** (`calculateAnnualSummary` over the source bills, before any adjustment), `nextBillTotal` is the same over the next-year bills, `subscriptionDelta = nextBillTotal − sourceBillTotal`, `carriedTotal = Σ items[].amount`, `firstInvoiceTotal = max(0, nextBillTotal + carriedTotal)` (the floor mirrors `getHouseholdFinancials`), and `remainingCredit = max(0, −(nextBillTotal + carriedTotal))`, the part of a carried credit the new year's bills do not use (shown as "$Y remains" and preserved at the following rollover by the rule above).
- The preview labels its baseline explicitly: "2026 bills" (what the household was billed for 2026 subscriptions) versus "2027 bills" (next year's subscriptions at their new prices). It never compares an unpaid balance with an annual billed cost, and it shows **Subscriptions vs 2026** separately from **Carried from 2026** so a catch-up charge is never hidden inside a price change.
- Every computed number shows its formula on demand; nothing is written until Start.

### The page (`/year-end-review`, `src/app/views/YearEndReview/YearEndReviewView.jsx`)

- A page under the app shell, not a dialog. Four steps with a step indicator; each step validates on Next and shows errors inline; Back never loses input. Steps 1–3 can be skipped only through the explicit "Nothing changed" confirmations (below).
- **Step 1 — Bills.** Every source bill, defaulting to **Unchanged**, with a one-click **No price changes this year**. Per row: Price changed (new price in the bill's frequency, first month billed at it, the computed True-up or Service Credit with formula and per-member split, amount overridable), Ended (last month of service, the computed Service Credit, "left out of 2027"), and **Next year's price** for every bill (default current or new price). A price change from January shows the hint "This bills all twelve months at the new price."
- **Step 2 — New subscriptions.** Empty by default and fast to skip. A row: name, price, frequency, website (optional, for the logo), members, first month of service; shows the late-start True-up and the next-year bill it creates.
- **Step 3 — Member charges.** Per household, the existing deferred Usage Charges prefilled (read-only, "already recorded") and **Add charge** with the existing Usage Charge validation (positive amount, description required, member of the household). Charges added here are recorded in the batch, not immediately.
- **Step 4 — Review & start &lt;year&gt;.** Per household: 2026 bills, 2027 bills at their new prices with the subscription delta, carried items grouped by member with the household credit (and its detail) at the household level, the carried total, the first-invoice total; each household expandable; Edit links back to Steps 1–3. Then the year label (from `suggestNextYearLabel`, editable, duplicate-checked with `isYearLabelDuplicate`), a plain-language statement of exactly what Start will do (counts of True-ups, Service Credits, member charges, new bills, price changes, ended bills, households carrying, and the two documents written), and **Start &lt;year&gt;**.
- **Nothing changed** is a fast path that requires three explicit confirmations: no closing-year bill changes, no new subscriptions, no additional member charges. "No price changes" alone does not skip Steps 2 and 3. It skips the editing of Steps 1–3; Step 4 still shows every existing deferred charge, Credit, next-year price (defaulting to the current price), and resulting total, with links back to edit. It persists no new financial records merely because a row was prefilled; prefilled deferred Usage Charges are carried once, never re-recorded.
- Entry points: Settings → Billing year (**Review & start &lt;next year&gt;**, replacing Start New Year and its label dialog) whenever the guard below finds no successor and the status permits, regardless of the date; and a Dashboard prompt card shown when **no successor exists AND (the year is Closed OR today is on or after November 1 of the year's four-digit numeric label)**. Cases: no successor, Open or Settling, before November 1 → no card (Settings still offers the review); no successor, Open or Settling, November 1 or later (including any later year) → card; no successor, Closed, any date → card; Archived → never; a successor exists → never, whatever the status or date. A label that is not four digits ("2026-27") has no date trigger and uses the Closed trigger only. "Today" is the browser's local date (`localDateString()`), the convention incurred dates already use.
- Guard: `findSuccessorYear(billingYears, sourceYear, sourceEvents)` returns a successor when (1) the source year's `billingEvents` contain a `YEAR_ROLLED_OVER` or `YEAR_CARRIED_FORWARD` event (its `toYear`), or (2) a billing year exists whose numeric label is greater than the source's numeric label; otherwise null. The review is offered only when there is no successor and the source status is `open`, `settling`, or `closed` (never `archived`). The guard runs at entry and again at Start after `_loadYearsList()`, and the commit precondition is the final check. When a next year exists the page says so and points to recording new charges in the open year (ADR 0007).

### Draft (local only)

- Draft state is component state mirrored to `localStorage` under `ffb.yearEndReview.v1.<uid>.<sourceYearId>`; every read and write is wrapped in `try/catch`; the billing-year document is never written before Start (ADR 0008 untouched).
- Shape: `{ draftVersion: 1, reconciliationId, userId, sourceYearId, sourceLabel, nextYearLabel, sourceFingerprint, savedAt, bills: { [billId]: { change: 'unchanged' | 'price_change' | 'ended', newAmount, firstMonth, lastMonth, override, nextYearAmount } }, newBills: [{ rowId, billId, name, amount, frequency, website, members, firstMonth, override }], charges: [{ rowId, memberId, amount, description, incurredDate }], confirmations: { noBillChanges, noNewSubscriptions, noExtraCharges } }`. `reconciliationId` is allocated once when the draft is created and never changes through restoration or retries. Derived amounts (True-ups, Service Credits, items, totals) are never stored.
- `validateDraft(raw, { userId, sourceYear, bills, familyMembers })` treats storage as untrusted input: the shape and every field type are checked; a draft for another user, another source year, or another `draftVersion` is incompatible and discarded with a message; referenced bills and members are resolved against current data (a bill that no longer exists drops its row with a notice; a member no longer on a bill or in the household drops that charge with a notice); amounts and months are re-validated; derived amounts are recomputed. Restoration preserves every `rowId`, `billId`, and the `reconciliationId`.
- A restored draft whose `sourceFingerprint` differs from the current fingerprint is **stale**: inputs are kept, derived amounts are recomputed, and a banner says what changed in plain terms (counts of payments, charges, credits, bills, members that differ) and that totals were recomputed. The administrator must pass through Step 4 again before Start.
- When storage is unavailable the draft degrades to in-memory state with a notice ("This draft is not saved on this device"); the page works unchanged.
- **Discard draft** is offered on every step and confirms. The draft is cleared only after confirmed success or explicit discard; a failed or uncertain commit retains it.
- When a next year was created elsewhere during the flow (detected at Start or on reload by the guard), the page says so, names the year, keeps the draft, and offers Discard draft or Open &lt;year&gt;; it never commits.

### Rollover (`BillingYearService.createYear(yearId, { reopenedAdjustmentIds, reconciliation })`)

- **Compatibility boundary.** `reconciliation` omitted (`undefined`) is the legacy call: the same two documents with the same content (the carry, the seeds, the markings, the `YEAR_CARRIED_FORWARD` event), no new records, no new events, now committed through the version-checked transaction of § Versioned year-document writes (the new year `rev: 1`, the source `rev + 1`) instead of a bare `writeBatch`. Its **business outcome** is preserved and asserted by its existing tests; the earlier promise that its `writeBatch` and tests stay byte-for-byte unchanged is withdrawn (decided 2026-11-05, D-A). `reconciliation` present, even `{}` or a "Nothing changed" payload, is the review path. The two are distinguished by `'reconciliation' in options`, not by truthiness.
- Payload: `{ reconciliationId, sourceYearId, sourceFingerprint, trueUps: [...bill-level inputs...], serviceCredits: [...], usageCharges: [...], billOverrides: { [billId]: nextYearAmount }, endedBillIds: [...], newBills: [...], confirmations }`; the service rebuilds every record through the pure library (never trusting amounts from the page) and rejects a payload that fails validation before any write.
- Order inside the enqueued commit: (1) build the appended source-year arrays (`owedAdjustments` + True-ups as `deferred` + Service Credits + new Usage Charges); (2) `buildCarryForwardSummary` over **that** updated state with `reopenedAdjustmentIds`; (3) `applyCarryForwardToPriorYear` with the deterministic id factory, which flips every carried deferred charge (new True-ups included) to `carried_forward`; (4) `buildNewYearData` with the derived next-year bills and the itemized carry; (5) append events; (6) commit both documents atomically. True-ups are therefore never persisted as `deferred`.
- Events on the source year: `TRUE_UP_RECORDED` per True-up `{ trueUpId, billId, billName, basis, calc, amount: gross, memberCount, memberIds, reconciliationId }`; `SERVICE_CREDIT_RECORDED` (existing payload plus `basis`, `calc`, `reconciliationId`); `USAGE_CHARGE_RECORDED` (existing payload plus `reconciliationId`); `YEAR_CARRIED_FORWARD` (existing, plus `reconciliationId` and `itemCount`) when anything carries; always `YEAR_ROLLED_OVER { toYear, reconciliationId, trueUpCount, serviceCreditCount, usageChargeCount, priceChangeCount, endedCount, newBillCount }`. Events on the new year: `YEAR_CREATED_FROM_REVIEW { fromYear, reconciliationId, householdCount, carriedTotal }`; `BILL_PRICE_ROLLED_OVER { billId, billName, previousValue, newValue, billingFrequency, fromYear }` per bill whose next-year price differs from its source price; `BILL_CREATED` per new bill with `source: 'year_end_review'` and `firstMonth`. `BILLING_EVENT_LABELS` gains `TRUE_UP_RECORDED: 'True-up recorded'`, `YEAR_ROLLED_OVER: 'Year rolled over'`, `YEAR_CREATED_FROM_REVIEW: 'Year created'`, `BILL_PRICE_ROLLED_OVER: 'Price set at rollover'`.
- The review path writes the source-year document even when the source year is Closed (the existing rollover exception) and even when nothing carries (the `YEAR_ROLLED_OVER` event), so any concurrent rollover changes the source document.
- Permitted source statuses: `open`, `settling`, `closed`; `archived` is rejected. Service Credits on a Closed source year are permitted under ADR 0009's same-commit disposition rule; because a Closed year's households are all Settled, the review's Service Credits there can only produce a payment-backed surplus, which the carry disposes in the same commit, and the service asserts before commit that no household of a Closed source year reads Outstanding after the appended records. On an Open or Settling source year a Service Credit may lower a household's shortfall or turn it into a carried surplus; it never raises a shortfall (invariant (c) in § Itemized carry).
- `billDeferredCharges` on a household holding a deferred True-up (the standalone-primitive case) bills it like any other deferred Usage Charge; a test confirms it.

### Commit guard and recovery (decided 2026-11-04: a Firestore transaction for the review path)

- The review path commits through `runTransaction`. Inside the callback, in this order: `tx.get` the destination (`users/<uid>/billingYears/<yearId>`) and `tx.get` the source year **before any write**; if the destination exists, throw `NEXT_YEAR_EXISTS` (never overwrite); recompute `computeSourceFingerprint(normalizeYearData(sourceSnapshot))` from the transaction's own read and compare it with the **fixed** `reconciliation.sourceFingerprint` the administrator reviewed; if they differ, throw `STALE_SOURCE` (a new fingerprint is never accepted silently, on any attempt or retry); only then build the source-year payload from the transaction's fresh read plus the appended records and events (never from in-memory state) and `tx.set` both documents. The transaction still runs inside `this._saveQueue.enqueue(...)`, behind any pending save of the active year, and a failure is captured and re-thrown after the queue settles exactly as today. Both documents carry `rev` (§ Versioned year-document writes): the source's expected `rev` is the one the transaction read, the destination is created with `rev: 1`, and the rules check both independently. The legacy path commits through the same version-checked transaction with its own payloads.
- **The callback is free of external side effects**: it mutates no service state, emits no notifications or toasts, allocates no ids (every id is computed before the transaction from the draft), and performs no reads or writes other than its two `get`s and two `set`s, because the SDK may run the callback more than once under contention. State, the draft, and the UI change only after the returned promise settles.
- **Rules and permissions.** `firestore.rules` grants `allow read, write` on `users/{userId}/billingYears/{yearId}` on the owner condition alone, with no field, key, or diff constraints, so the transaction's reads (including a `get` of the absent destination) and its two `set`s are permitted for the signed-in owner with no rules change. The implementation ticket re-verifies this against `tests/rules/firestore.rules.test.js` with a transaction-shaped test (get absent destination, get source, set both) before claiming it.
- `computeSourceFingerprint(state)` is a pure, key-order-independent hash over `status`, `bills` (id, name, amount, billingFrequency, members), `familyMembers` (id, name, linkedMembers), `payments` (id, memberId, amount, type, reversed), `creditAdjustments`, `owedAdjustments`, and `billingEvents.length`. Settings and template fields are excluded. It is computed once when Step 4 is shown and stored on the draft as the reviewed fingerprint; refreshing the preview replaces it only after the administrator sees the new totals.
- **Outcomes.** `NEXT_YEAR_EXISTS` — the destination exists; the service reads its `billingEvents`: a `YEAR_CREATED_FROM_REVIEW` event carrying this draft's `reconciliationId` means the operation already completed (switch to it, clear the draft, no second commit); otherwise it was created elsewhere (keep the draft, show the created-elsewhere state, never commit). `STALE_SOURCE` — nothing written; the page refreshes the preview from the current state, shows what changed, and requires Step 4 to be confirmed again before Start is enabled. `COMMIT_FAILED` — a definite failure before or at commit (`aborted` after the SDK's own retries, `failed-precondition`, `permission-denied`, `invalid-argument`, a thrown validation error): nothing written, the draft kept, Retry offered. `COMMIT_UNCERTAIN` — `unavailable`, `deadline-exceeded`, `unknown`, `internal`, a cancelled request, or a non-Firestore error after the commit was sent: the service reads the destination and the source year's events back before any retry and classifies the result as completed (clear the draft, switch), created elsewhere (keep the draft, stop), or not committed (Retry allowed with the same `reconciliationId`, ids, and reviewed fingerprint).
- **Offline.** A Firestore transaction cannot run offline (`tx.get` rejects with `unavailable`, "client is offline"); the page treats it as `COMMIT_UNCERTAIN` only when the commit was sent, otherwise as `COMMIT_FAILED` with the message "You're offline. Nothing was saved; your draft is kept." Retry re-runs the guard and the transaction; it never falls back to a batch or to a write without the preconditions.
- Repeated attempts therefore produce one logically identical rollover: deterministic ids mean a completed commit is recognizable, the destination precondition means it is never overwritten, and the fingerprint means source records are never appended twice.
- **Tests** (`tests/react/lib/BillingYearService.test.js`, with a `runTransaction` mock that can replay the callback, fail the commit, or reject after committing): a stale preview (a payment recorded after the preview → `STALE_SOURCE`, nothing written); two sessions creating the same label (the second gets `NEXT_YEAR_EXISTS` and does not overwrite); a retry after `COMMIT_FAILED` producing exactly the same records and ids; a successful commit whose response is lost (`unavailable` after commit → read-back finds `YEAR_CREATED_FROM_REVIEW` with the reconciliation id → completed, no second commit); the callback replayed by the SDK producing no duplicate records or side effects; a definite failure leaving no destination document and no source change; and the legacy call still using `writeBatch`.
- **Writers of `users/<uid>/billingYears/<yearId>` and what the transaction does and does not protect.** The client writes the year document from four places: `save()` (a full-document `setDoc` without merge of the active year, built from in-memory state that was loaded once by `getDoc`; nothing subscribes with `onSnapshot`), `setYearStatus()` (a merge `setDoc` of `status` and the **whole** in-memory `billingEvents` array), the legacy `createYear()` (`writeBatch`: `set` of the new year and, when anything carries, `set` of the source from memory, after a duplicate check against the in-memory years list), and `loadData()` (the first year's `setDoc` when none exists). The Cloud Functions write `publicShares`, `shareTokens`, `disputes`, `mailQueue` and rate-limit documents, never the year document. A tab running a build deployed before this feature keeps running it until reload (`UpdateToast` polls `version.json` every 60 s and shows a banner; it does not reload). The rules allow every one of these writes on the owner condition alone. **Enforceable today:** the reviewed path never overwrites an existing next year and never commits over a source that changed after the preview, against **any** writer, because the transaction's own reads decide (reviewed-versus-reviewed yields exactly one commit; a stale preview yields `STALE_SOURCE`). **Not enforceable today:** (1) *legacy-versus-reviewed*: a pre-feature tab whose years list predates the new year passes its duplicate check and its `writeBatch` overwrites the new year with a legacy-shaped document and, if anything carried, the source with its stale arrays (the True-ups, review Service Credits, `carry_forward` records and rollover events vanish from the source while the new year's seeds, if they survive, still hold the money); (2) *stale-save-after-rollover*: any tab (old or new build) that loaded the source before the rollover and then saves (recording a payment is enough) replaces the source with its pre-rollover arrays, with the same loss, and a stale `setYearStatus` replaces the events (the successor guard's second test, the higher numeric label, still blocks a second review). No client code can stop a pre-feature tab, and the current rules cannot tell a stale write from a fresh one. On its own the transaction therefore gives no universal no-overwrite guarantee. **Decided 2026-11-05 (D-A, option 2):** every writer of the year document becomes version-checked and Firestore rules enforce the version (§ Versioned year-document writes), staged so that the rules are in force before the reviewed rollover ships. After stage 2 the two sequences above are refused by the rules for every build, and the commit-guard tests assert the refusal; until stage 2 lands, the tests assert the current behavior so the gap is visible, not claimed fixed.

### Versioned year-document writes (decided 2026-11-05: D-A, option 2)

- **Contract.** Every write to `users/<uid>/billingYears/<yearId>` carries an integer `rev` and is version-checked twice: by the client, which writes only through a `runTransaction` that reads the document, compares the stored `rev` with the `rev` it loaded (`expectedRev`) and writes `rev = expectedRev + 1`; and by the Firestore rules below, which require `create` to carry `rev == 1` and `update` to carry `rev == resource.data.rev + 1`. The writers covered: `save()` (the full document), `setYearStatus()` (which stops being a direct merge write and becomes a full-document write of the same payload through the queue), the legacy `createYear` (both documents), the reviewed rollover (both documents, in addition to its destination-exists and fixed-fingerprint checks, which stay), and the first-run creation in `loadData()`. All of them run through the `SaveQueue`, so a status change is serialized behind a pending save and rapid same-tab edits commit in order; the service's in-memory `rev` advances only when a commit resolves, so each queued write expects the right value. `rev` rides `buildSavePayload`'s allowlist (ADR 0008 amended) and `normalizeYearData` (`year.rev`, `undefined` for an unversioned document). `save:success` is emitted only after the transaction resolves.
- **Stale write outcome.** When the transaction's read shows a `rev` other than `expectedRev` (or, for an unversioned document, a different fingerprint, below), nothing is written; the service raises `STALE_DOCUMENT`, the queue stops accepting writes for that year (every later enqueue rejects with the same outcome), and the app enters a read-only **Reload required** state: a blocking dialog says the year changed in another tab or device, names the time of the tab's last successful save, lists the unsaved changes as the billing events appended since the loaded snapshot (label, amount, member, time), and offers **Copy details** and **Reload**. A `permission-denied` from the rules (a stale client against enforced rules, or any write the rules refuse) produces the same state. Nothing is merged automatically: the app never applies a fresh `rev` to stale data, and the saving indicator shows *Not saved*, never a success tick, for a write that did not commit. The unsaved state is also kept on the device (`localStorage` key `ffb.unsaved.v1.<uid>.<yearId>`, try/catch, the event list plus the serialized payload) so it survives the reload; after the reload the Dashboard shows *You had unsaved changes from <time>* with the same list and Copy details until dismissed. Frame `#fx-dashboard-stale-*`.
- **Lost response and offline.** A transaction that fails before commit leaves `expectedRev` unchanged and the next save retries with the same expectation. When the outcome is uncertain (`unavailable`, `deadline-exceeded`, `unknown`, `internal`, a dropped connection after the commit was sent), the next transaction's read settles it: stored `rev == expectedRev + 1` with this tab's own fingerprint means the write committed (adopt the `rev`, continue); stored `rev == expectedRev` means it did not (retry); anything else is `STALE_DOCUMENT`. A transaction cannot run offline; the save is reported as *Not saved* with the offline message and retried on the next save, never downgraded to a plain write.
- **Rules (narrow; `firestore.rules`, the one change this epic makes to it).** Reads are unchanged (owner only). Writes:

  ```text
  match /billingYears/{yearId} {
    function isOwner() { return request.auth != null && request.auth.uid == userId; }
    function reqRev() { return 'rev' in request.resource.data && request.resource.data.rev is int; }
    function hasRev() { return 'rev' in resource.data && resource.data.rev is int; }
    function sameCreatedAt() { return !('createdAt' in resource.data) || request.resource.data.createdAt == resource.data.createdAt; }
    allow read: if isOwner();
    allow create: if isOwner() && reqRev() && request.resource.data.rev == 1;
    allow update: if isOwner() && (
         (reqRev() && hasRev() && request.resource.data.rev == resource.data.rev + 1)      // a versioned write advances by exactly one
      || (reqRev() && !hasRev() && request.resource.data.rev == 1 && sameCreatedAt())      // the first versioned write of an unversioned document
      || (!reqRev() && !hasRev()));                                                         // STAGE 1 ONLY: an unversioned write onto an unversioned document (old tabs during the rollout); removed at stage 2
    allow delete: if false;
    match /disputes/{disputeId} { allow read, write: if isOwner(); }                         // unchanged
  }
  ```

  `sameCreatedAt()` is what keeps a create-shaped rollover payload (whose `createdAt` is the server time) from overwriting an existing *unversioned* year through the migration branch; a genuine first versioned save carries the stored `createdAt`. A `writeBatch` or transaction that touches two year documents fails as a whole when either document fails its check. **Delete:** no client or server path deletes a year document, and none is planned; the rule denies it explicitly, so a year can only be archived. The disputes subcollection and every other collection are untouched.
- **Migration of unversioned documents.** Existing documents have no `rev`. A document becomes versioned by its first version-checked write, which sets `rev: 1` and may proceed only when the transaction's read equals the loaded snapshot by **document fingerprint** (`computeDocumentFingerprint(normalizeYearData(doc))`: a key-order-independent hash of every allowlisted field, status, label and timestamps; `updatedAt` alone is insufficient because the current `setYearStatus` does not bump it). A mismatch means an unversioned writer changed the document after the load: `STALE_DOCUMENT`, reload. Treating a missing `rev` as zero is explicitly not the rule. No migration script is needed: after stage 2 every remaining unversioned document is versioned by its owner's first write under the same check, and until then an old tab can only touch a still-unversioned document, which the fingerprint check catches.
- **Staged rollout, rules before the reviewed rollover.** *Stage 1* (two tickets, rules first): deploy the rules above, which accept versioned writes and still accept unversioned writes onto unversioned documents; then ship the client that writes `rev` through transactions with the stale-write recovery. *Stage 2*: remove the stage-1 disjunct so every write must carry `rev`, after the stage-1 client has been live for at least one release cycle with the update banner (`UpdateToast`, 60-second poll) and the stage-1 rules tests have been green on `main`; a tab that loaded before stage 2 is refused on its first write (`permission-denied`) and recovers through the same Reload required state. The reviewed rollover (#606, #607, #609) ships only after stage 2 is in force: #607 is natively blocked on the stage-2 ticket.
- **Legacy `createYear`.** The business outcome is unchanged (same two documents, same carry, seeds, markings and event, apart from `rev`), but it commits through the version-checked transaction inside the queue (source at `expectedRev`, destination created with `rev: 1`, which the rules also refuse when the destination exists). The promise that its `writeBatch` and tests stay byte-for-byte unchanged is withdrawn; its tests are updated for `rev` and the transaction mock and keep every business assertion.
- **Limitations the rules cannot cover (stated, not claimed away).** (1) Admin SDK writers bypass rules. No Cloud Function writes a year document today; `tests/functions.test.js` gains a source guard asserting none does, and any future server writer must implement the same read-and-increment contract inside a transaction. (2) The rules verify `rev == stored + 1` at commit time, not that the writer read the document; a writer that guessed the current `rev` could still overwrite. Every writer in this repository reads first, and the guard above covers the server. (3) The rules cannot compare content, so the unversioned-document fingerprint check is client-side only and therefore not enforced against old tabs during stage 1; stage 2 closes that. (4) The `users/<uid>` document's `activeBillingYear` pointer is not versioned (last writer wins is acceptable for a pointer). (5) Rules cannot tell the reviewed rollover from the legacy one; both are held to the same checks.
- **Tests.** Rules (`tests/rules/firestore.rules.test.js`): a versioned write at `stored + 1` succeeds; the same `rev` or `+ 2` fails; `create` with `rev: 1` on an absent document succeeds and on an existing versioned document fails; a create-shaped payload onto an unversioned document fails on `createdAt`; an unversioned write onto a versioned document fails; an unversioned write onto an unversioned document succeeds at stage 1 and fails at stage 2 (the suite runs both rule files); a batch holding one stale document fails entirely; two concurrent transactions on the same document leave exactly one committed; `delete` is denied; the disputes rules are unchanged. Service (`tests/react/lib/BillingYearService.test.js`, `tests/react/lib/SaveQueue.test.js`): rapid same-tab edits commit in order with consecutive `rev`s; a status change waits behind a pending save; two sessions saving the same year leave one `STALE_DOCUMENT` with no write; the first-run race (two tabs creating the same first year) leaves one document; a lost response is classified by the next read; offline and `permission-denied` produce the Reload required state with the unsaved list persisted; the legacy `createYear` produces the same documents as before apart from `rev`; the reviewed rollover carries `rev` on both documents and keeps its destination-exists and fingerprint checks. Old tabs are simulated by writing without `rev` against each rules stage.
