# The Year-End Review creates the next year; True-ups are the bill-level Usage Charge; the carry is itemized (amends ADR 0005)

A billing year is billed in advance, so anything that changes a subscription's price partway through the year, starts partway through the year, or ends partway through the year leaves members under- or over-billed for the remaining months. Before #322 nothing modeled that gap: editing a bill's amount rewrote what was billed (rejected by ADR 0005), charged twelve months at the new price, and raised present-tense owed so every household on the bill went Outstanding. `addBill` in the closing year did the same for a subscription that started mid-year. `buildNewYearData` copied the closing year's prices verbatim into the new year, and `carry_opening` seeds carried one netted number per household that `invoice.js`, `share.js`, and the Cloud Function folded into a total with no line items.

This record settles four things.

## 1. A True-up is the bill-level variant of a Usage Charge

A **True-up** is a deferred, bill-level debit for the months a bill's members were under-billed: a price increase (`basis: 'price_change'`) or a subscription that started mid-year (`basis: 'late_start'`). It is stored as `kind: 'usage_charge'`, `status: 'deferred'`, split among the bill's members with the same cents-and-remainder helper `recordServiceCredit` uses (extracted into one shared function), and each per-member record carries `billId`, `billName`, `basis`, a `calc` object (`fromMonthly`, `toMonthly`, `firstMonth`, `months`, `computed`, `overridden`), and a `trueUpId` shared by the records of one True-up. A Usage Charge record without `basis` is a `'member'` charge.

This completes ADR 0005's grid: Service Credit already has a bill-level default and a per-member variant; Usage Charge now has the same two shapes, and both share the split. The payoff is that every helper keyed on `usage_charge` + `deferred` already does the right thing: ignored by owed (`getHouseholdFinancials`), carried by `buildCarryForward`, transitioned by `applyCarryForwardToPriorYear`, billable off-cycle by `billDeferredCharges` and `selectBillableCharges`. `src/lib/calculations.js` has no diff. A new `kind` would have forced edits to half a dozen high-risk helpers, where one miss silently drops or double-counts money.

A price decrease or a bill that ended mid-year is the mirror: a **Service Credit** with the same `calc` and `basis: 'price_change'` or `'ended'`. Ended bills are left out of the new year.

## 2. The Year-End Review is the way a new billing year is created

"Start new year" was a label prompt with no review step. It becomes a four-step page (`/year-end-review`): bills (price changed, ended, or unchanged, plus every bill's next-year price), new subscriptions, member charges, then a per-household preview and **Start &lt;year&gt;**. The page is the only entry to `createYear` from the UI. Settings → Billing year opens it; the Dashboard offers it when no next year exists and the year is Closed or the calendar has reached November 1 of the year's label.

Nothing is persisted until Start. Draft state is component state mirrored to `localStorage` (keyed by user and source year, every access in `try/catch`), so ADR 0008's save allowlist is untouched and the billing-year document is never written mid-review. The draft is versioned and bound to the source year and a fingerprint of the reviewed source state; restored storage is untrusted input that is validated, resolved against current members and bills, and recomputed before it is shown.

**The closing year's bills are never edited.** The 2026 bill list stays what was billed in advance; True-up and Service Credit records carry the explanation; the new year's bill history records the price set at rollover.

## 3. One atomic rollover, computed over the updated prior-year state

`createYear(yearId, { reopenedAdjustmentIds, reconciliation })` appends the review's True-ups, Service Credits, and new Usage Charges to the prior year, computes the carry over that updated prior-year state, seeds the new year with the overridden prices, the new bills, and the itemized carry, and marks the prior year, in one commit inside the `SaveQueue`. In the rollover's in-memory calculation the new True-ups are deferred; because creation and carrying commit together, their first persisted state is `carried_forward`. Existing deferred charges shown in the review are reused, not appended again.

An omitted `reconciliation` is the legacy call: the same two documents with the same content (same carry, seeds, markings and event), now committed through the version-checked transaction below; its business outcome is preserved and its tests keep every business assertion, but the earlier promise that its `writeBatch` and tests stay byte-for-byte unchanged is withdrawn (decided 2026-11-05). An explicit, possibly empty, `reconciliation` is the review path: it always writes the source year (a `YEAR_ROLLED_OVER` event with the reconciliation id) and itemizes whatever already carries.

**The review path commits through a Firestore `runTransaction`** (decided 2026-11-04). A `writeBatch` cannot express "the next year must not exist" or "the source must be what I reviewed" in the web SDK, so on its own it is last-writer-wins when two sessions create the same year and blind to source changes after the preview. The transaction reads the source and destination documents before writing, recomputes the source fingerprint from its own reads on every attempt and compares it with the fixed fingerprint the administrator reviewed, stops with a stale-source outcome when they differ (a new fingerprint is never accepted silently), stops and reconciles when the destination exists (never overwrites it), and writes the same two documents through the same `buildSavePayload` allowlist. **What this guarantees, exactly:** the reviewed commit itself never overwrites an existing next year and never lands on a source that changed after the preview, whichever writer made the change. **What it does not guarantee:** that nothing overwrites the two documents afterwards. The year document has other full-document writers, `save()` (a `setDoc` without merge from state loaded once by `getDoc`, with no snapshot listener), `setYearStatus()` (a merge that rewrites the whole `billingEvents` array from memory) and the legacy `createYear` `writeBatch`, and a tab running a pre-feature build keeps those writers until it reloads. A stale `save()` after the rollover, or a pre-feature tab's legacy rollover for the same label, replaces the source's appended records and events or the new year itself, and the current rules cannot tell a stale write from a fresh one. The transaction alone therefore claims no universal no-overwrite guarantee.

**Every writer of the year document is version-checked, and the rules enforce it** (decided 2026-11-05, D-A option 2). Each write carries an integer `rev`; the client writes only through a transaction that reads the document and advances the `rev` it loaded by one, and narrowly scoped Firestore rules require `create` at `rev == 1` and `update` at `rev == stored + 1`, deny `delete`, and, during a stage-1 window, still accept from pre-feature tabs an unversioned create and an unversioned update onto an unversioned document, so old tabs fail only where they could overwrite a versioned one. The writers covered are the full-document save, the status change (which joins the save queue instead of writing a merge directly), the legacy year creation, the reviewed rollover, and the first-run creation. A stale client running this build fails safely: nothing is written, the app enters a read-only Reload required state that lists the unsaved changes (the events and payload differences since the last successful save) and keeps them on the device when it can, and no failed save is ever shown as successful or silently re-stamped with a fresh version. A tab still running a pre-feature build cannot be given that behavior; at stage 2 its denied writes are logged and lost, an accepted and owner-acknowledged window bounded by the staged rollout and the update banner. Existing unversioned documents are versioned by their first checked write only when the stored document still matches the loaded snapshot by fingerprint; a missing version is never treated as zero. The rollout is staged (rules, then client, then enforcement), and the reviewed rollover ships only after enforcement. Protection, and the window in which a tab still running the pre-feature build can lose a write, begin per document with its first versioned write at stage 1, not at stage 2; the owner acknowledges that window before the client deploys and again before enforcement. A completed rollover is recognized by its reconciliation id **and** a fingerprint of the payload that produced it, so a second tab that restored the same draft and diverged is never told its different entries were committed; a lost response is settled by a per-attempt write identity stored in the document, not by comparing payloads that contain server timestamps. The rules cannot constrain Admin SDK writers (none write the year document; a functions source guard keeps it so), cannot verify that a writer actually read before writing, and cannot compare content; those limits are stated in `specs/year-end-review.md` § Versioned year-document writes together with the rules text, the migration rule, the delete rule and the tests. This amends ADR 0008: `rev` joins the save allowlist, and the rules now constrain the shape of a year-document write. The transaction callback has no external side effects: no state mutation, no notifications, no network calls other than its own reads and writes, because the SDK may run it more than once. The Firestore rules permit the transaction's reads and writes today, and after the change below they also check the version of both documents.

Record ids are deterministic (`oadj_tu_<sourceYear>_<trueUpId>_<memberId>`, `oadj_sc_<sourceYear>_<basis>_<billId>_<memberId>`, `oadj_uc_<sourceYear>_<rowId>`, `cadj_carry_<nextYear>_<primaryMemberId>`, `coadj_<sourceYear>_<primaryMemberId>`), stable through draft restoration and retries. Completion is evidenced on both documents: the new year's `YEAR_CREATED_FROM_REVIEW` event and the source year's `YEAR_ROLLED_OVER` event carry the reconciliation id, so an uncertain commit is settled by reading them back before any retry.

## 4. The carry is itemized

`carry_opening` seeds gain `items[]`, a snapshot of each carried source: `{ sourceId, kind, basis, memberId, billName, description, amount }`, built in `billing-year.js` from the ids `buildCarryForward` already returns. A household Credit is a net position (ADR 0001), so it is one negative item attributed to the household (its `memberId` is the household contact, its `kind` is `'credit'`, its `sourceId` is the deterministic `carry_forward` record); Service Credits from the review that produced it are listed beneath it as non-additive `detail[]`. Invariant: the signed top-level items sum to the seed's signed `amount` within `CREDIT_EPSILON`. Seeds written before this change have no `items` and render as one "Carried from &lt;year&gt;" line. The review passes its own Service Credits to the item builder for the detail (the carry seam returns no Service Credit ids), and `buildCarryForwardSummary` preserves a carried credit that exceeds the year's bills, in `billing-year.js` only.

**Incoming credit is money; a Service Credit is not.** `getHouseholdFinancials` floors the bill-derived owed at zero and derives the household's Credit from `netContribution`, so two different things vanish in that floor. An over-large Service Credit (credits above the bills) is a reduction of charges with no payment behind it; the existing rule forfeits the excess and the review keeps it: $100 of bills, a $200 Service Credit and no payments carry nothing. A carried-in credit is money the household paid in an earlier year; when the new year's charges are smaller than it, the remainder is hidden by the floor, reads as no Credit, and would be dropped at the next rollover. The review path computes that remainder as the **unused incoming credit** `U = max(0, incomingCredit − max(0, grossOwed − serviceCreditTotal + incomingCharge))`, exactly the part of the household's ledger balance the floor hides, and carries it. It is carried **without a `carry_forward` disposition record**, because that record subtracts from `netContribution` and the incoming credit was never in `netContribution`: disposing $132 of unused incoming credit that way would turn a Settled household into one Outstanding by $132. Instead the source year's `carry_opening` seeds are stamped `carriedForwardTo` with their status unchanged, so the source year's owed, credit and status are identical before and after. Payment-backed credit keeps the existing disposition record. The invariants that follow, property-tested: top-level items sum to the seed, detail sums to the credit item, the seed equals deferred charges less (payment-backed credit plus unused incoming credit) and nothing else, and after the commit every source household has no Credit as the carry computes it and a shortfall that is zero when it carried payment-backed credit and unchanged otherwise; a refund re-opened by an active `not_received` is the deliberate exception, owed back in the source year and never carried, so it still shows there as a Credit after the rollover (ADR 0003). A consequence of the frozen owed formula is that an unused incoming credit can only carry, never be refunded in the year it went unused, because the Record Refund gate reads the payment-backed Credit and a refund record would create a shortfall; whether v1 accepts that (the member page says what remains) or the owed formula changes to count a carried-in credit in Net Contribution is the open product decision D-C (#617), since ADR 0004 says refund is the fallback that keeps a credit from being trapped.

**An existing Service Credit on a changed bill is never netted silently** (decided 2026-11-05, D-B option 1). A credit recorded from the Bills tab is one record per member with no period, no basis and no group id, so the data cannot tell an earlier outage credit from the cancellation the review is looking at, nor a one-member credit from a bill-level one. The review therefore asks the administrator, per record, whether it covers this change, with no default; shows the record's date, reason, member and amount and the resulting credit per member; stores the answers by the record's own id without inferring a group or a purpose; never alters the existing credit; nets only the amount that actually covers this change for that member, offers "Covers in part" with an entered amount for every record because a record smaller than the share can still be mixed-purpose, and never assumes a whole record applies (a record larger than the remaining share cannot be answered Covers at all). A member whose share is fully covered gets no new record, an ended bill still leaves the next year, a reduced bill keeps its new price, and the applied ids and amounts are kept in the rollover audit either way. Answers are revalidated against the record and the change's coverage, so a restored draft never reuses a stale answer. There is no override of zero: a zero recorded amount comes only from a December ending or from full netting.

The new year's share page, invoice preview, and invoice email show a **Carried from &lt;year&gt;** section with each line attributed to its member and totaled at the household, in plain language ("Northline Mobile went from $180 to $198 a month in July. Your share of six months: $12.00."). The Cloud Function builds share data independently of `share.js` (`functions/billing.js`, `resolveShareToken`), so it gets the same `carriedItems` builder and the same cache↔function parity test, and it ships before the client renders the section.

## Closed-year rollover

The review may run from a source year that is **Open, Settling, or Closed**, when no next year exists; never from an Archived year. `createYear` already writes the prior-year document regardless of status (it is not `_guardReadOnly`-guarded), and `applyCarryForwardToPriorYear` is the existing rollover-time append exception to ADR 0007.

- **Debit-only records** (True-ups, Usage Charges) are born deferred and carried in the same commit. They never touch the closed year's owed, so its settlement metrics are identical before and after Start.
- **Service Credits** (decrease, ended) lower owed in the closing year. On a Closed year they are permitted only because the resulting Credit is disposed by the carry in the same commit: every household of a Closed year is Settled, so a reduction there can only turn Settled into a carried surplus; the closed year never gains an undisposed Credit, no household of a Closed year reads Outstanding afterwards (asserted before commit), and the close gate is untouched. On an Open or Settling year a Service Credit may lower a shortfall or produce a surplus; it never raises a shortfall. The accepted consequence is that the closed year's owed total is lower after rollover, visible in its billing events; this is ADR 0007's forward correction, with the record living on the year whose bill it explains.
- No settlement precondition is added: a household still Outstanding in an Open or Settling source year carries its True-ups like any other; its 2026 balance is unchanged.
- Reopening a closed year is never required for the review and is not performed by it.

## Amends

- **ADR 0005**: a Usage Charge is "per-member, or split across a bill's members as a True-up"; the bill-level split helper is shared by Service Credits and True-ups; `carry_opening` seeds carry `items[]`.
- **ADR 0007**: rollover-time appends to a Closed year include the review's records, under the same-commit disposition rule above.
- **ADR 0008**: `rev` joins the full-document save allowlist, and the Firestore rules for `users/{uid}/billingYears/{yearId}` constrain every write to advance that version by one (and deny delete); React remains the sole writer.

## Considered options

- **A new `kind: 'true_up'`**—rejected: forces edits to every helper keyed on `usage_charge` + `deferred`, each a chance to drop or double-count money.
- **Editing the closing year's bill amounts or adding late-start bills to its `bills[]`**—rejected by ADR 0005 for the same reasons as before, plus twelve months at the new price and a false Outstanding on every household.
- **Keeping one netted `carry_opening` number**—rejected: a member's first invoice jumps with no explanation; the biggest trust risk in the feature.
- **A modal review**—rejected: four steps with tables do not fit a dialog, and phone dialogs are bottom sheets (Redesign 14).
- **Recording review Service Credits in the new year instead of the closed year**—rejected: the bill they explain may not exist in the new year (an ended bill), and the bill's own history would stop being honest.
- **Keeping `writeBatch` for the review path and checking for an existing next year before the commit**—rejected as insufficient: the check and the write are not atomic, so two sessions can still both pass it.
- **Client-side version checks without a rules change**—rejected (D-A): a tab running a pre-feature build keeps its unversioned writers until it reloads, and nothing client-side can stop it from overwriting the source or the new year; only the rules reach every build.
- **Treating a missing `rev` as zero during migration**—rejected: while unversioned writers are active, a document can change between the load and the first versioned save without the version revealing it; the first checked write requires the stored document to match the loaded snapshot by fingerprint instead.
